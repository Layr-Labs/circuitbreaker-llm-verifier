# A bounded-local fair cut of density strictly below 63893/500000

On the cubic tree with a specified perfect matching, there is an equivariant bounded-radius rule with independent labels, fair at each physical vertex, whose expected crossing count per vertex (counting each undirected edge once) is below c=63893/500000. Different edge types are not assumed to have identical crossing probability. Section 2 of proof.md proves the edgewise transfer to arbitrary finite subcubic graphs needed for the circuit argument.

The graph parameters are a=28/57, t=41/57 and T=-11/50. Fixed comparisons rederived by the packaged experiment are: the *actual initial majority* strictly above 1/2; the post-repair split energy strictly below c; and a relaxed finite-radius terminal majority strictly below 1/2, with all of its recurrence layers regenerated from the maximal boundary. There is no motif adjustment, asymptotic terminal fixed point assumption or score beyond the bound declared in bound.json.


## 1. A globally compatible Gaussian source, including branched neighborhoods

Contract the perfect matching to anchors of a loopless 4-regular multigraph Q. Its four labeled incidences are paired into two straight continuations, one physical vertex per pair. Its marked universal tree has two lines through every anchor. Set t=41/57, a=28/57, delta=t-a, so t^2+2a^2=1. We need the joint law on whole finite **branching balls**, not just agreement of covariances on two central lines.

Give anchors independent standard Gaussians Z. On an incoming oriented edge uv (source u), let B^0_uv=Z_u. For each further shell j+1, take t times the depth-j message arriving from the straight opposite direction at u plus a times each from the other two directions. Each B^j_uv has variance one by disjointness of its three predecessor half-trees. Different shells of that one oriented edge use disjoint distance layers. Set B^[J]_uv to the sum of shells 0,...,J-1 divided by sqrt(J); it has unit variance. The four incoming variables at a given root are iid standard normals, and any collections of inward-oriented descendant rows on disjoint forward half-trees are independent. All these finite rules are equivariant and local. In mean square the discrepancy of B^[J] from the t,a,a recurrence applied to its neighbors' B^[J] has norm sqrt(2/J), since only the first and next omitted shells survive. Along a diagonal subsequential finite-dimensional Gaussian limit the recurrence is exact and that half-tree independence persists. Equivalently each finite-dimensional covariance family is bounded and successively compact; the tree is countable. The resulting local laws are unique by the following recursive sampler.

Define the root anchor score R as the half-sum of its four incoming B modes. For any chosen forward direction with incoming mode B, the three modes at its source farther into this branch are independent normals before conditioning, constrained by B=t B_f+a(B_l+B_r). Conditional on B, they may be generated by two independent new standard normals Z,Y as follows: B_f=tB+sqrt(2)a Z, W_0=sqrt(2)a B-t Z, B_l=(W_0+Y)/sqrt(2), B_r=(W_0-Y)/sqrt(2). The backward mode into this site is determined by the earlier three incident modes, by the same t,a,a recurrence. Different new subtrees are conditionally independent given their entering modes: their full inward families were independent across disjoint half-trees, and the value conditioned on for each half is measurable there. Previously revealed upstream modes involve no additional data from these halves beyond those entering modes, again by the recurrence. This inductively determines the joint law on each finite tree.

Here is the two-real-state version used by the certificate; all transition coefficients are rational. Generate independent standard R,U shared by the two lines. Draw an independent V~N(0,1/2) for each line ell=+1,-1. For each initial ray on that line, set
```
D = a(R+ell U)/2 +/- aV,    E=((a+t)R+delta ell U)/2,    P=D+E.
```
At a fresh site draw W~N(0,1/2). Its anchor score is X=P-delta W. The straight continuing ray has new states
```
D_s=tD+2a^2 W,    P_s=tP+aD+2a^2 W.
```
For the perpendicular pair take another independent V~N(0,1/2); their two states are
```
D_o=a(D-tW) +/- aV,
P_o=tD+aP+(a^2-t^2-at)W +/- aV.
```
Conditional on the parent state (D,P) and innovations W,V, the remaining innovations in the three descendant half-trees are independent. To check the reduction, D=aB for the entering mode, the new forward B is tB+2aW, and the backward B is 2E-tB. The two perpendicular modes therefore sum to 2(D-tW), while the forward and backward pair sum to 2(E+aW). A ray on any line has E=(t times its line's pair sum + a times the other pair sum)/2 before the next site; applying this at the perpendicular line gives all formulas. Along one straight line, r_d=Cov(R,X_d)=(a+t/2)t^(d-1)+(d-1)a^2 t^(d-2)/2 (omit the final term at d=1). The independent-root-modes and the ray recursion restricted to the two lines are also used by the straight-line energy and crossed-line gain calculations below. In particular the limiting one-line Gaussian law is the same ergodic moving average. One may work throughout with the finite shell scores and finite free-boundary line windows, then approximate each fixed finite neighborhood to arbitrary accuracy. Threshold equalities at individual Gaussian inputs have probability zero, so joint threshold statistics on a fixed finite ball converge, without needing a numerical bound on J.

### 1.1 Exact line and crossing covariances
The whole-tree law is invariant under arbitrary marked-tree automorphisms (by equivariance of finite shell laws and the unique limiting recursion). For consecutive vertices at distance d>=1 on one straight line, take either one as the reference root. Coefficients of the shared R in the first site's D,P are a/2 and (2a+t)/2. Repeated straight updates send (D,P) to (t D,t P+a D) at the linear coefficient level. As X=P-(t-a)W with a fresh independent W, the endpoint covariance with R is
    r(d) = (a+t/2) t^(d-1) + (d-1) a² t^(d-2)/2,
omitting the term with d−1 at d=1; r(0)=1. Same-line distances are therefore completely specified. Every finite consecutive covariance is positive definite: the first site's R and then each next site's own independent W appear triangularly with nonzero diagonal coefficients. This also proves strict positive definiteness of principal blocks.

Write h(d)=a t^(d-1). The coefficient of shared U in the endpoint at distance d is ell[r(d)-h(d)] by the same recurrence (at the first site it is ell t/2). Two axes have opposite signs of ell and share only R,U. Thus for positions at distances i,j on two distinct crossing lines their covariance is
    r(i) r(j) - (r(i)-h(i))(r(j)-h(j))
      = h(i)r(j)+h(j)r(i)-h(i)h(j).
Within one line use the preceding distance formula (also for opposite rays); the recurrence covariance agreement does not treat the two crossing lines as independent.

## 2. Initial line-minimum policy and the first-pass energy

Each physical vertex lies on a C line and has one M pin. A split means its three incident bits are not all equal; equivalently the split count is the NAE objective. For fixed raw matching bits, any line C-edge assignment minimizes split count exactly when its split set is a minimum vertex cover of the consecutive disagreement graph. One direction holds because every disagreement edge requires a split term at one endpoint; conversely the outside-cover unsplit vertices force consistent exterior edge bits and all remaining edges may be assigned arbitrarily. On an individual disagreement path the minimum cover size equals its maximum matching size by pairing consecutive vertices (and every selected edge requires a distinct covered vertex). A left-to-right greedy maximum matching is optimal: if the earliest disagreement edge in the path is omitted, replace the optimum's first incident edge by it, or add it if no incident edge was used, and induct on the suffix. Writing d_i for the disagreement indicator, the greedy acceptance bit satisfies g_i=d_i(1-g_(i-1)). Substitute this 13 times; the alternating partial sum of the preceding-run products through length 13 is an upper bound pointwise because the remaining term is nonpositive. Averaging the stationary law, with A_j(T) the probability of j consecutive disagreements,
    c1 <= S2(T)+A3(T)-A4(T)+A5(T)
            -(A6-A7)(T)-(A8-A9)(T)-(A10-A11)(T)-(A12-A13)(T).
Here S2(T)=P(X_0>T,X_2<=T)=A1-A2: the two adjacent disagreements have XOR equal to the endpoint disagreement; stationarity makes its two orientations equally likely. In a finite window greedy vs full-line counts differs only within its initial disagreement component; almost surely finite components suffice upon taking expectations and growing window size.

For the actual bit choices use the least optimizer of the C bits. Existence for finite windows follows from submodularity, and the following exact recursion shows that the limiting choice stabilizes locally. The incoming straight-ray context is a trit, the difference “optimal cost at 0 minus optimal cost at 1” on the supplied edge, clamped to [-1,+1]. The raw matching bit b sends fixed trit 2b−1 into the physical vertex, so a site transforms input x by f(x,b)=sign(x+2b−1). Direct minimization over the two incident free bits at a physical vertex with fixed third bit is also the special case of the NAE trit rule in §5. Two consecutive identical raw pins send an identical extreme f(f(x,b),b)=2b−1 regardless of the boundary, so the directed context stabilizes once that ray has a repeated pair. Almost surely this happens on each fixed ray, as shown in §4. For a physical vertex at an anchor with two incoming messages x,y and original b, the two adjoining optimal C bits are [x+f(y,b)>0] and [y+f(x,b)>0] (the conditional minimum at bit 1 must strictly beat that at bit 0). This is consistent on each C edge because it uses the two contexts at its ends. One may implement bounded locality by truncating every such ray at a common depth L with a fixed external trit, and assigning each C edge according to its two independently evaluated directed contexts; both sides of an edge thus refer to the same assigned bit. For fixed L, the finite shell source in §1 is sufficiently close to the limiting law by compactness/uniqueness; the rate of exceptional cut runs need not be computed at growing graph scale.

#### Rule, nodes, weights and directed arithmetic

The standard Gauss-Legendre rule on [-1,1] uses roots of P_N, where P_0=1, P_1=x and (k+1)P_(k+1)=(2k+1)xP_k-kP_(k-1). For clarity, orthogonality follows from the Rodrigues derivative formula by k integrations by parts, and the recurrence follows by expanding it or comparing leading coefficients in the orthogonal basis. Polynomial division by P_N reduces degree <=2N-1 to a remainder of degree <N after integration, since the quotient is orthogonal to P_N. The quadrature weights are the integrals of the Lagrange basis for the roots; they equal the integrals of the squared basis, by the same division argument, and are positive. The Christoffel-Darboux identity (telescoping the three-term recurrence with squared norms 2/(2k+1)) gives at a root x the reciprocal weight on [-1,1] as (1-x^2)P_N'(x)^2/2. The identity (1-x^2)P_N'(x)=N P_(N-1)(x) at roots follows from the recurrence. After mapping to [0,1] the weight is (1-x^2)/(N^2 P_(N-1)(x)^2). Exactness for constants gives weight sum one.

The checker treats each hard-coded root location as an untrusted guess for an interval of width 2^-72. It proves by integer-scaled recurrence that P_32 changes sign at both ends of every bracket (opposite signs), verifies disjointness and counts 32 distinct real roots of a degree-32 polynomial. This accounts for every root, so no root-finder or trusted floating node is needed. It evaluates the displayed weight formula by intervals and checks positive weight bounds and a bracket of the exact weight sum. All interval operations enclose rational results with explicit floor/ceiling at scale 2^72; integer square roots enclose square roots. Pi uses Machin's identity and the alternating arctangent series; arcsine uses its arctangent half-angle identities, twice, followed by the same alternating series with the next term as error bound (argument at most 1/2 checked). The true correlation is in [-1,1], so intersecting with that interval is valid. Every integration term is enclosed and the analytic error added.
For an analytic derivative bounded by 512 on the ellipse of radius 3/2, the Laurent coefficient estimate via the Joukowski map bounds Chebyshev coefficient k>=1 by 2*512*(2/3)^k. Truncating after degree 63 incurs uniform error at most 2*512*(2/3)^64/(1-2/3). The integral and positive normalized GL32 rule each have norm one, so their difference is at most twice this outward error. ### 3.2 A third-order Gaussian orthant lower bound

    prod(v_i)/(2^n sqrt(det G)) E exp(L),
    L=H^T B H + l^T H - T^2 D/2,

## 4. The actual initial majority exceeds one half

It applies to the actual *least global* first-pass line minimizer. It does not insist that positive agreement C-edges stay 1 when both endpoints are split. The initial matching repair may turn a positive raw pin off (take 0 on ties) but never turns a negative raw pin on.

Conversely given a cover, unsplit vertices prescribe their C incidences consistently, because every disagreement edge is covered, so the minimum equals the sum of the component minimum cover sizes. In every minimizing assignment the split set is a minimum cover. Isolated vertices of the disagreement graph are never split.

For an odd path with 2k+1 vertices its unique minimum vertex cover is the k internal alternating positions, indices 1,3,...,2k-1 from either endpoint. Proof by induction: a matching of k pairs leaves the last endpoint uncovered in some optimum, and conversely deletion of an endpoint from a cover of size k forces its neighbor covered and leaves the same characterization recursively. ## 5. Simultaneous local erasure cannot increase the same edge-split energy

On a finite graph let S be the set of positive edges of the initial assignment and E(S)=sum_v NAE on its incident bits. E is a submodular set function (each NAE is submodular). For a patch B of allowed edge variables choose the least minimizer X_B among subsets of S agreeing with S outside B. It exists by meet/join closure. Its removal set is C_B=S minus X_B. For any union D of previously selected removals, submodularity gives
```
E(S minus (D union C_B)) <= E(S minus D)+E(S minus C_B)-E(S minus (D intersection C_B)).
```
The last set is feasible on the B patch: it differs from S only by deletions inside B. Thus the last two costs have nonpositive difference. Induction proves that the sum of split indicators cannot increase for **any simultaneously activated subcollection**, with arbitrary overlap. All patches are minimized against the same original S, not sequentially recomputed. This is a claim on edge-bit split energy, not an assertion that two unrelated vertex mincuts can be combined.
