# An eventual 4.8913N gate lower bound by softened crossing covers

For the fixed total language in “From cubic layouts to the norm-one language”, unrestricted full-$B_2$ circuits require at least $(48913/10000)N$ gates for every sufficiently large actual length $N$. The explicit remainder is zero.

The hard reference crossing-cover cost per vertex has upper limit $U=1020684112681/7812500000000$. A single edge labeling saves $1-\lambda$ on a set of expected density at least $p=78895046267/18432000000000$. The interpolation theorem uses $\lambda=35/71$ and incurs factor $R=80089/80080$ (and $R=1$ for all $1/2\le\lambda\le1$). Thus the limiting width and its implied limiting gate coefficient are
$$a_0=R[U-(1-\lambda)p]=5844501392789112861861/45485440000000000000000,\qquad 1+1/(2a_0)=28587221392789112861861/5844501392789112861861>48913/10000.$$
Choose $a$ strictly between $a_0$ and $1/[2(48913/10000-1)]$. The slack absorbs all uniform errors for sufficiently large graphs, giving edge cutwidth at most $a|V|+O(1)$ on every connected simple cubic graph. The final reduction proves the circuit claim.

## Layout from softened anchors

Let $H$ be a finite simple cubic graph on $n$ vertices. Each vertex has an anchor bit $b_v$ and each edge a bit $x_e$. Let $Q(b,x)$ charge a vertex $1$ if its incident edge bits split, $\lambda$ if they are unanimous and disagree with its anchor, and zero otherwise. Set $G_\lambda(b)=\min_x Q(b,x)$. For $1/2\le\lambda\le1$, if $M$ bounds $G_\lambda$ along a nested single-vertex anchor order, then
$$
 \operatorname{cw}(H)\le M+o(n)
$$
uniformly over graphs and anchor orders. For $3/8\le\lambda\le1/2$ the estimate is $\operatorname{cw}(H)\le R M+o(n)$ with $R=1+9(1-2\lambda)_+^2/[16\lambda(3-2\lambda)]$.

**Endpoint states.** The vertex cost is the minimum over an auxiliary bit $s_v$ of
$\operatorname{NAE}(s_v,(x_e)_{e\ni v})+\lambda|s_v-b_v|$. Both terms are submodular: their cardinality increments are nonincreasing. Sum and partial minimization preserve submodularity by applying the lattice inequality to optimizers. Thus $Q$ is submodular in $(b,x)$. Least minimizing edge states exist by closure under meet. At two ordered anchor states, the meet and join of optimal edge states are optimal at the earlier and later states, respectively. Taking the least optima makes them increasing. At the initial and final anchors the unique edge states are all zero and all one since $\lambda>0$.

Fix a transition; $D$ is the graph of changed edges with isolated vertices omitted. Count by $P,A,B$ the vertices with split lists at both endpoints, only at the destination, and only at the source. The vertices trivalent in $D$ are precisely those changing from three zeros to three ones. Let their number be $Z$. Among them omit the changing anchor if present and let $Z_s,Z_d$ count the remaining trivalents paying the mismatch at the source and destination, respectively. Thus $Z\le Z_s+Z_d+1$. For $0\le p\le1$, endpoint optimality gives
$$
 M\ge P+pA+(1-p)B+\lambda[(1-p)Z_s+pZ_d].                       \tag{1}
$$

**Costs of splitting edge groups.** Give every edge of $D$ one label, later assigned an independent uniform priority. At time $p$ a label has flipped with probability $p$. A degree-one vertex has its usual interpolation cost. At a degree-two vertex exactly one fixed incident bit remains, making this vertex type $A$ (fixed zero) or $B$ (fixed one). If its two changing edges have one label, its split probability is respectively $p$ or $1-p$. With two labels these probabilities become $2p-p^2$ and $1-p^2$, each an extra $p(1-p)$. At a trivalent with $k\ge2$ incident labels the split probability is $1-p^k-(1-p)^k=k p(1-p)$ since $k=2$ or $3$. One label costs zero. Thus the expected split count is at most
$$
 P+pA+(1-p)B+p(1-p)S,
$$
where $S$ counts divided degree-two vertices with weight one and divided trivalents with weight equal to the number of their labels. Vertices in $P$ need at most their fixed budget of one.

**Counting independent cycles.** Use integers $L\ge2$ and $J=L^2$. Give each original connected component of $D$ of size at most $L$ a single label; its contribution to $S$ is zero. In a larger component $C$ let $\beta(C)=|E|-|V|+1=(Z_C-\ell_C)/2+1$ be its cycle rank, where $\ell_C$ counts degree-one vertices. Form the connected components $K$ of the graph induced only by vertices trivalent in $D$. In particular, a path through a degree-two vertex does not connect two trivalents in this induced graph. Every cycle outside the union of these $K$'s has a degree-two vertex on it. Replace one such vertex by two pendant copies, one on each of its edges. The rest of the cycle connects the two sides, so connectivity is preserved. There is one extra vertex, hence the cycle rank drops by one. No induced $K$ changes. Repeat until every remaining edge outside the $K$'s is a bridge. After contracting each connected $K$ to one vertex and deleting its internal edges the remainder is a tree, so the number of divided degree-two vertices is exactly $\beta(C)-\sum_K\beta(K)$. After assigning labels and identifying its two copies, each such vertex costs at most one unit of $S$.

Leave each $K$ of at most $L$ vertices intact. In each larger $K$, repeatedly delete a degree-three vertex whose deletion preserves its current internal connectedness. Original edges have no loops. A vertex of degree three in the current $K$ has all three of its original edges there and therefore has no outside incidence. Deleted vertices are independent, since a previously deleted neighbor would leave internal degree at most two. Each deletion reduces $\beta(K)$ by two. The connected remainder is a cactus: in a nonbridge block that is not a cycle, minimum internal degree is two and some internal degree is at least three. A vertex with three internal edges has no edge outside the block, so deleting it would preserve connectedness, contradicting termination. A singleton remainder causes no difficulty. Reinsert the three incidences of every deleted vertex using three distinct pendant copies. This leaves a connected cactus attached along exactly the original boundary. Identifying these three copies after assigning labels will cost at most three units of $S$, for total at most $3\beta(K)/2$ in this large $K$. All previous bridges outside the induced components remain bridges, since each induced component has merely been replaced by a connected graph with its same attachment points.

The expanded connected graph therefore has a tree decomposition of bag size at most $\max(L,3)$. Use one vertex bag for each small $K$. For each large replacement cactus take the usual block-cut tree, decomposing a cycle $v_0,\ldots,v_{r-1}$ along the path of triples $\{v_0,v_i,v_{i+1}\}$, $1\le i\le r-2$, a bridge by its endpoint pair, and a singleton by its one vertex. Glue at shared vertices with singleton bags. The remaining bridges of the expanded graph join these decompositions along a tree; for each bridge introduce its pair bag, adjoining bags containing its two endpoints. Isolated remaining vertex pieces use singleton bags.

A connected piece of $m$ vertices in this expanded graph has a separator of at most $\max(L,3)$ splitting it into pieces of size at most $m/2$. Restrict the tree decomposition to the piece, assign each vertex to one containing bag and take a weighted tree centroid. The bags containing a remaining vertex are connected, and adjacent vertices share a bag. Consequently every component after deleting the centroid bag lies in a single centroid branch. Apply these separators recursively until pieces have at most $J$ vertices. The pieces processed in any size range $(2^jJ,2^{j+1}J]$ are disjoint since size halves down the recursion. Thus there are at most $2m/J$ separators. The expanded graph over all original components has at most $3n$ vertices, since each divided original vertex has at most three copies. The union of the removed separator vertices therefore has size $O(nL/J)$. Give all edges incident with each surviving connected piece a common label and assign edges with both ends removed individual labels. No edge can join two such pieces. Each label occurs on at most $3J$ edges, and only removed separator vertices can meet multiple labels in the expanded graph. Charge at most three units for each of them, then identify the divided original vertices with the costs just proved.

Applying this cycle-breaking and separator decomposition to any subgraph with at most $n/L$ large components $C$ and large induced trivalent components $K$ bounds its split-cost units by
$$
 S\le\sum_{C\ {\rm large}}\left(\beta(C)-\sum_{K\subset C}\beta(K)
                       +\tfrac32\sum_{K\subset C,\ |K|>L}\beta(K)\right)
                 +O(nL/J). \tag{2}
$$

**Two-phase interpolation.** Fix a transition along the monotone least-minimizer sequence of $Q$. All counts below refer to the union $D$ of original changed-edge components with more than $L$ vertices; smaller original components receive one label each and contribute zero split fluctuation above the full-graph baseline $P+pA+(1-p)B$. Write $z_d,z_s$ for destination- and source-paying trivalents in $D$ omitting the changing anchor, and $z=z_d+z_s+O(1)$ for all trivalents in $D$. Let $a$ count $A$ vertices of $D$ with unchanging anchor one (source-mismatched) and $b$ count $B$ vertices of $D$ with unchanging anchor zero (destination-mismatched). Every $P$ vertex in $D$ has degree one in $D$, since at degree two the single fixed bit makes one endpoint unanimous. Switching all changing edges of $D$ to their destination values at the source optimizer, or the reverse at the destination optimizer, affects no vertex outside $D$, and the single changing anchor alters the cost difference by at most $2\lambda$. Thus the endpoint costs $M_0,M_1$ differ by $O(1)$ on $D$, giving
$$ B-A=\lambda(z_d-z_s+b-a)+O(1), \tag{3} $$
and $(1-p)M_0+pM_1\le M+O(1)$ pays at least $P+pA+(1-p)B+\lambda[p(z_d+b)+(1-p)(z_s+a)]-O(1)$. By time reversal (exchanging $0\leftrightarrow1$, source and destination, $A\leftrightarrow B$, $z_d\leftrightarrow z_s$, $a\leftrightarrow b$, and reversing the edge order), assume $B\ge A$.

Let $\mathcal K_L$ be the union of induced trivalent components $K$ of $D$ with $|K|>L$ ($z_L$ trivalents total) and $\partial\mathcal K_L$ the $\delta_L=\sum_{|K|>L}d(K)$ changed edges between $\mathcal K_L$ and $V(D)\setminus V(\mathcal K_L)$. Set $D_{\mathrm{out}}=D\setminus(E(\mathcal K_L)\cup\partial\mathcal K_L)$, dropping isolated vertices. Every induced trivalent component in $D_{\mathrm{out}}$ has size at most $L$. For each connected component $C'$ of $D_{\mathrm{out}}$, let $m(C')$ count its trivalents of $D$, $A(C')=A_0(C')+A_{\mathrm{sh}}(C')$ and $B(C')=B_0(C')+B_{\mathrm{sh}}(C')$ count its $A$ and $B$ vertices split into those ($A_{\mathrm{sh}},B_{\mathrm{sh}}$) touching $\partial\mathcal K_L$ and those ($A_0,B_0$) with all changing edges in $C'$, and $\ell^{\mathrm{orig}}(C')$ count degree-one vertices of $D$ in $C'$. The degree-one vertices of $C'$ form the disjoint union $A_{\mathrm{sh}}(C')\cup B_{\mathrm{sh}}(C')\cup\ell^{\mathrm{orig}}(C')$, so $\ell(C')=A_{\mathrm{sh}}(C')+B_{\mathrm{sh}}(C')+\ell^{\mathrm{orig}}(C')$, $\beta(C')=(m(C')-\ell(C'))/2+1\ge0$, and $m(C')\equiv\ell(C')\pmod2$. Give $C'$ one label ($S(C')=0$) if $|V(C')|\le L$; if $|V(C')|>L$, the decomposition above divides no trivalent of $C'$ and divides $S(C')=\beta(C')-\sum_{K\subset C'}\beta(K)\le(m(C')-\ell(C'))/2+1$ degree-two vertices with $O(|V(C')|L/J)$ separator cost.

Let $\mathcal F$ be the union of components $C'$ of $D_{\mathrm{out}}$ with $A(C')+S(C')\le B_0(C')$. In Phase 0, hold $D'=D\setminus E(\mathcal F)$ at zero and flip labels of $\mathcal F$ from zero to one by independent uniform priorities on $[0,1]$. At time $p_1\in[0,1]$, vertices of $B_{0,F}$ split with probability $1-p_1$ (plus $p_1(1-p_1)$ if divided in $S(\mathcal F)$), vertices of $A_F$ have their outside bit at zero and split with probability $p_1$ (plus $p_1(1-p_1)$ if divided), vertices of $B_{\mathrm{sh},F}$ have fixed bit one and $\partial\mathcal K_L$-bit zero so stay split at cost one, and trivalents in $m_F$ cost zero up to $O(|V(\mathcal F)|L/J)$ separators. Since $A(\mathcal F)+(1-p_1)S(\mathcal F)-B_0(\mathcal F)\le0$, expected splits throughout Phase 0 are at most $P+B+O(nL/J)\le M+O(nL/J+1)$.

Throughout Phase 1 (holding $\mathcal F$ at one and flipping labels of $D'$ from zero to one on $[0,1]$), vertices of $B_{0,F}$ and $m_F$ stay unanimous one (cost zero), vertices of $A_{0,F}$ stay split (cost one), vertices of $A_{\mathrm{sh},F}$ have fixed bits $(0,1)$ and one edge in $\partial\mathcal K_L\subset D'$ (degree-one $P$-type in $D'$, cost one), and vertices of $B_{\mathrm{sh},F}$ have fixed bits $(1,1)$ and one edge in $\partial\mathcal K_L\subset D'$ (degree-one $B$-type in $D'$, split probability $1-p$); both $A_{\mathrm{sh},F}$ and $B_{\mathrm{sh},F}$ add to the degree-one count $\ell'$ of $D'$. Every connected component $C^*$ of $D'$ has size $>L$: any edge of $D'$ meets a large $K\subset\mathcal K_L$, a component $C'\in\mathcal G$ of $D_{\mathrm{out}}\setminus\mathcal F$ of size $>L$, or a component $C'\in\mathcal S$ of $D_{\mathrm{out}}\setminus\mathcal F$ of size $\le L$ (which touches $\partial\mathcal K_L$ since its enclosing component in $D$ had size $>L$). Thus $D'$ has at most $n/L$ components. Applying (2) to $D'$ with $\beta(C^*)=(Z_{C^*}-\ell'_{C^*})/2+1$ and $\beta(K)=(|K|-d(K))/2+1$ for $|K|>L$ bounds expected splits at time $p\in[0,1]$ of Phase 1 by $E_1(p)=P+pA+(1-p)B-(1-p)(B_{0,F}-A_F)+p(1-p)S'+O(n/L+nL/J)$, with $S'\le 3z_L/4+(m_S+m_G)/2-\delta_L/4-\ell'/2+O(n/L+nL/J)=3z/4-H'/4+O(n/L+nL/J+1)$ and $H'=3m_F+m_S+m_G+\delta_L+2\ell'\ge0$.

Partition the nontrivalents of $D$, $\delta_L$, and $\ell'=(A_{\mathrm{sh},F}+B_{\mathrm{sh},F})+\ell_S^{\mathrm{orig}}+\ell_G^{\mathrm{orig}}+\ell_{\mathrm{pure}}^{\mathrm{orig}}$ across $V(\mathcal F)$, $V(\mathcal S)$, $V(\mathcal G)$, and $V_{\mathrm{pure}}=V(D)\setminus(V(\mathcal K_L)\cup V(D_{\mathrm{out}}))$. On $\mathcal F$, $2(B_F-A_F)\le 2(B_{0,F}-A_F)+3m_F+3(A_{\mathrm{sh},F}+B_{\mathrm{sh},F})$. On each $C'\in\mathcal S$, $S(C')=0$ and $C'\notin\mathcal F$ give $2(B_0(C')-A(C'))\le-2$, while $\beta(C')\ge0$ gives $B_{\mathrm{sh}}(C')+\ell^{\mathrm{orig}}(C')\le\ell(C')\le m(C')+2$; adding gives $2(B(C')-A(C'))\le m(C')+(A_{\mathrm{sh}}(C')+B_{\mathrm{sh}}(C'))+2\ell^{\mathrm{orig}}(C')$. On each $C'\in\mathcal G$, $C'\notin\mathcal F$ gives $B_0(C')\le A(C')+S(C')-1\le A(C')+(m(C')-\ell(C'))/2$ by integrality; doubling gives $2(B(C')-A(C'))\le m(C')+(A_{\mathrm{sh}}(C')+B_{\mathrm{sh}}(C'))+2\ell^{\mathrm{orig}}(C')$. On $V_{\mathrm{pure}}$ (subscripts $1,2$ for degree in $D$), its part of $\delta_L+2\ell'$ is $3(P_{1,\mathrm{pure}}+A_{1,\mathrm{pure}}+B_{1,\mathrm{pure}})+2(A_{2,\mathrm{pure}}+B_{2,\mathrm{pure}})\ge 2(B_{\mathrm{pure}}-A_{\mathrm{pure}})$. Summing and using (3) gives $H'+2(B_{0,F}-A_F)\ge 2(B-A)-O(1)=2\lambda(z_d-z_s+b-a)-O(1)$.

For $1/2\le p\le1$ and $\lambda\ge3/8$, $H'\ge0$ gives $S'\le 3(z_d+z_s)/4+O(n/L+nL/J+1)$, so $(M_0+M_1)/2-E_1(p)\ge(p-1/2)(B-A)+(1-p)(B_{0,F}-A_F)+(\lambda/2-3p(1-p)/4)(z_d+z_s)+\lambda(a+b)/2-O(n/L+nL/J+1)\ge-O(n/L+nL/J+1)$ since $3p(1-p)/4\le3/16\le\lambda/2$. For $0<p\le1/2$ and $\lambda\ge3/8$, taking $\theta\in[0,1]$ times the lower bound on $H'$ plus $(1-\theta)$ times $H'\ge0$ gives $S'\le(3/4-\theta\lambda/2)z_d+(3/4+\theta\lambda/2)z_s+\theta\lambda(a-b)/2+\theta(B_{0,F}-A_F)/2+O(n/L+nL/J+1)$. In $E_1(p)-[(1-p)M_0+pM_1]$, the coefficients of $B_{0,F}-A_F\ge0$, $a$, and $b$ are $(1-p)(\theta p/2-1)\le0$, $-\lambda(1-p)(1-\theta p/2)\le0$, and $-\lambda p(1+\theta(1-p)/2)\le0$. Set $\theta=\min(1,(4\lambda-3p)/(2\lambda p))\in[0,1]$, making the $z_s$ coefficient $(1-p)[p(3/4+\theta\lambda/2)-\lambda]\le0$. Where $\theta<1$ ($4\lambda/(3+2\lambda)<p\le1/2$ when $\lambda<1/2$), the $z_d$ coefficient is $p(3/2-3p/2-\lambda/p)\le p(3/2-\sqrt{6\lambda})\le0$. Where $\theta=1$ (all $p\in[0,1/2]$ when $\lambda\ge1/2$, and $p\le4\lambda/(3+2\lambda)$ when $3/8\le\lambda<1/2$), the $z_d$ excess is at most $p[(1-p)(3/4-\lambda/2)-\lambda]z_d\le(3/4-3\lambda/2)_+^2/[4(3/4-\lambda/2)]z_d$ with $z_d\le M/\lambda$. This yields $\operatorname{cw}(H)\le R M+o(n)$ with $R=1+9(1-2\lambda)_+^2/[16\lambda(3-2\lambda)]$ for $3/8\le\lambda\le1$, so $R=1$ on $[1/2,1]$ and $R=80089/80080$ at $\lambda=35/71$.

**Obtaining a single layout.** Use factor $R$ (and $R=1$ on $[1/2,1]$). Take $L=\max(2,\lceil n^{1/16}\rceil)$ and $L$ equal time intervals in each of Phase 0 and Phase 1. Each independent priority influences the split count at a mesh point by at most twice its edge count, with maximum $O(J)$ and sum at most $2|D|\le3n$, giving variance $O(nJ)$. The edge count in each mesh interval has expectation at most $|D|/L$ and variance $O(nJ)$. By Chebyshev and a union bound, with positive probability for all large $n$, all $4L+2$ counts across both phases differ from their means by at most $n^{3/4}$, since $LJ/\sqrt n=o(1)$. Continuous priorities avoid mesh endpoints almost surely, and inside one interval the split count rises by at most twice its edge count. Thus every edge prefix has at most $RM+O(n/L+nL/J+n^{3/4}+1)=RM+o(n)$ split vertices; concatenate these orders across transitions.

Finally order the original vertices by the index of their median incident edge, breaking ties arbitrarily. At a proper nonempty vertex prefix let $j$ be the largest included median. Its crossing edges with index below $j$ are charged to distinct excluded endpoints, at which each is the lowest edge and the list splits just before $j$. Those above $j$ charge distinct included endpoints, where they are highest edges and the lists split just after $j$. The two split sets differ only at the ends of edge $j$, and edge $j$ itself contributes at most one crossing. This adds at most three to the split bound, proving the theorem.

## From the reference tree to every finite cubic graph

Write $F(b)$ for the smallest vertex cover of the edges with differently colored endpoints. For fixed vertex bits $s$, the smallest value of the star sum $\sum_v\operatorname{NAE}(s_v,(x_e)_{e\ni v})$ equals $F(s)$. Every crossing edge has a paid endpoint, and a cover lets all its outside vertices set their incident bits consistently. In particular the softened functional of the layout lemma is
$$G_\lambda(b)=\min_s[F(s)+\lambda |s\mathbin\triangle b|].$$
It is submodular by partial minimization of the joint submodular star cost and disagreements. Complementing all bits preserves its value. Altering one anchor changes the value by at most $\lambda$.

**Exact descent.** For a finite degree-$d$ cover $\pi:\widehat H\to H$ and anchors repeated from below, the upstairs softened optimum equals $d$ times the downstairs optimum. Lifting gives one inequality. Average arbitrary upstairs vertex and edge values in each fiber, obtaining numbers in $[0,1]$. At each base star the difference between its largest and smallest averaged entries is at most the average of the upstairs star ranges, by the local bijection on incidences. Threshold all averages at the same uniform point of $[0,1]$. The expected star cost is the range and the expected mismatch with each fixed base anchor is exactly the upstairs mismatching fraction. The minimum downstairs is bounded by the expected rounded cost. This proves equality.

**Gaussian ordering.** For a submodular complement-invariant function $G$ on bits and a centered unit Gaussian vector with covariance $C$, let $f_C(t)=\mathbb E G(1[X_i<t])$. Increasing the off-diagonal covariances decreases $f_C(t)$ under entrywise comparison of two covariance matrices. To verify this, interpolate linearly between the matrices. The derivative of a Gaussian integral against a smooth function is half the covariance derivative paired with the expected Hessian, by differentiation of the density followed by integration by parts. Smooth the threshold in the multilinear extension of $G$ and take its sharp limit. For each unordered coordinate pair the factor multiplying the covariance increment is the bivariate boundary density at $(t,t)$ times the conditional expectation of $\Delta_{ij}G=G_{11}-G_{10}-G_{01}+G_{00}\le0$. Variances are fixed. Positive-definite approximation by mixing both covariances with the identity extends the result to singular matrices by continuity at each marginal threshold.

Also $f_C(t)\le f_C(0)$ for all $t$. In the positive-definite case differentiation gives $f'_C(t)=\phi(t)\sum_i g_i(t)$, with $g_i(t)$ the conditional mean of the first difference $G_{i=1}-G_{i=0}$ given $X_i=t$. In this conditional law the other tests become $W_j<(1-C_{ij})t$, where $W_j=X_j-C_{ij}X_i$ are independent of $X_i$ as a vector. Increasing $t$ increases these test bits in this common coupling. Submodularity makes the first difference nonincreasing. Complement symmetry gives $g_i(-t)=-g_i(t)$, so it is nonpositive for $t\ge0$. Use the same marginal limit at singular covariances.

**Construction of the keys.** For a vertex $v$ of the universal cubic tree $T$, take the unit row
$$h_v(z)=\sqrt{(1-2\alpha^2)/(1+\alpha^2)}\,\alpha^{d(v,z)},\qquad 0<\alpha<1/\sqrt2.$$
There are $3\cdot2^{i-1}$ vertices at distance $i\ge1$. For centers at distance $j\ge1$, projecting each $z$ to their connecting path gives two endpoint contributions each with transverse generating series $1/(1-2\alpha^2)$ and $j-1$ interior contributions each with series $(1-\alpha^2)/(1-2\alpha^2)$. Thus their scalar product is $\alpha^j[(j+1)-(j-1)\alpha^2]/(1+\alpha^2)$. It approaches $r_j=(1+j/3)2^{-j/2}$. In particular the limiting covariance matrices on every finite ball are positive semidefinite and consistent. Truncate rows at radius $R$ and renormalize. For fixed $\alpha$ their $\ell^2$ error tends uniformly to zero as $R$ grows, by the geometric squared tail.

For any finite connected simple cubic $H$, take a finite cover of girth larger than $4R+2D+10$, where $D$ exceeds every witness radius and walk length used below. Such covers exist. Collapse a spanning tree to identify the fundamental group with the free group on the oriented nontree edges (reverse edges denote inverses). A nonbacktracking closed walk represents a nonidentity element, as its lift to the universal tree is a reduced path of positive length. For each of the finitely many closed walks of length at most the desired girth, choose its nonempty reduced word. Realize the successive letters as edges from position 0 through the last position on a finite line: for each generator its required forward or inverse steps form a partial bijection, since the word is reduced. Extend each partial bijection to a permutation. The word moves 0, so its image in this finite group is nonidentity. Take the product of these finite images and use its regular action as the voltage cover, with identity on spanning-tree edges. All selected closed walks act without fixed points, as required.

On this cover the truncated radial rows are defined by graph distance, and have tree norm. With independent unit Gaussians on columns they give a unit Gaussian reference field. Add independent tie noise by multiplying the field by $\sqrt{1-\delta^2}$ and adding $\delta$ times independent unit Gaussians. On the base graph collapse the square entries of each tree row above $v$ fiberwise under the universal covering, taking a square root per physical column. This row is independent of the lift chosen since deck transformations preserve fibers. Its coordinates also equal the norms of the truncated upstairs row over fibers of the finite cover. Take Gaussian linear combinations with independent base Gaussians and add base tie noise by the same normalization. The covariance of the resulting base field repeated on the cover dominates that of the upstairs field entrywise. This follows by Cauchy–Schwarz in each column fiber and positivity of extra noise correlations on repeated base vertices. The cover identity and Gaussian comparison give
$$|H|^{-1}\mathbb E G_{\lambda,H}(1[X<t])\le |\widehat H|^{-1}\mathbb E G_{\lambda,\widehat H}(1[\widehat X<0])$$
for every $t$. The preceding common-threshold inequality was applied upstairs. Every fixed tested ball upstairs has the tree covariance of the truncated rows. First choose $\alpha$ sufficiently close to its limit, then $R$ large and $\delta>0$ small. The joint laws on each tested finite ball converge to the specified Gaussian wave. Sign events on these balls have boundary probability zero because individual coordinates have continuous distributions. Therefore one can obtain all finite probability estimates below within any prescribed tolerance, uniformly in the physical graph and the covering degree.

**Independent branches of the limiting wave.** Set $\rho=r_1=2\sqrt2/3$. Given the root score $x$, the first named neighbor has law $N(\rho x,1/9)$. At a vertex of value $b$ reached from a parent of value $a$, the two forward values are $\sqrt2 b-a/2\pm\eta$, with $\eta\sim N(0,1/12)$. The sum follows from the eigenfunction identity proved below. The half-difference has variance $(1-r_2)/2=1/12$ and vanishing covariance with every score outside the two forward subtrees, including the vertex and parent, by equality of distances. Contrasts at different branching vertices are uncorrelated: for nested vertices the deeper contrast is orthogonal to both coordinates of the earlier contrast, and for disjoint ones the same distance argument applies. Joint Gaussianity makes these contrasts independent. The root's two remaining neighbors obey the same formula after its first named neighbor has been sampled, and the remaining forward innovations in the three branches are independent conditional on the root and all three neighbor scores. These identities justify the products of conditional probabilities in the witness calculation.

**From expectations to all prefixes.** If the limiting calculation below gives $G_\lambda/n\le s_0$ in expectation, the choices just described give expected base cost at most $(s_0+\epsilon)n$ at every threshold, for any fixed $\epsilon>0$. At fixed $R$ one independent Gaussian source affects at most the $O(2^R)$ vertices within radius $R$, and a tie source affects one. Bounded differences (or resampling variance) gives variance $O_R(n)$ for this cost and for each key-interval count. Partition the standard Gaussian law into finitely many quantile intervals each of mass at most $\epsilon$, including its two tails. Chebyshev and a union bound give a realization with cost at most $(s_0+2\epsilon)n$ at every finite boundary and at most $2\epsilon n$ vertices in any interval, for all sufficiently large $n$. The two infinite boundaries have zero cost. Independent tie noise gives distinct physical keys almost surely. Between boundaries, each changed bit costs at most $\lambda$, so the ordering of physical vertices by key has every soft prefix at most $(s_0+4\epsilon)n$. All parameters precede the graph order. Single-cell lookups evaluate at the monotone endpoint of `[yl,yh]`; 2D lookups apply `lower_y` on the bounding $x$-rows followed by monotone $x$-interpolation (zero outside $[0,2)^2$). Analytic $B_0(a,b)=H(rb+a/2)$ is evaluated directly, with quarter-step subdivision on the initial cells of $B_1$ and $S(B_0)$, adaptive quarter/half-step meshes on $(U,U)$ and $T$, and zero-crossing truncation/insertion at $h_{\max}=\lfloor(3b/2+a/\sqrt2)/r\rfloor$ in $(U,U)$ and $c_p=\lceil(ri-7j/2)/r\rceil$ in $V$ and $VU_0$.

At $n=48$, the four lower numerators are $204011953695$, $130843781187$, $81407805552$, and $57106737168$ over $110592000000000=10^9\cdot48^3$, giving $p=78895046267/18432000000000$.

## From cubic layouts to the norm-one language

Write $c$ for the declared coefficient and $g=1/(2(c-1))$. The ordering proved above gives a fixed $a<g$ and a constant $C$ such that every connected simple cubic graph $H$ has cutwidth at most $a|H|+C$. The threshold for the graph theorem is absorbed into $C$. All logarithms below are binary.

**The language.** Reject lengths $N<16$. Otherwise take $t=2^{\lfloor\log_4 N\rfloor}$ and let $m$ be the largest odd integer at most $(N-t)/(t+1)$. Read $t$ bits for the low coefficients of a monic polynomial $R(W)$ of degree $t$, then $m$ for those of a monic $Q(Z)$ of degree $m$, in ascending degree. Reject $m<1$ or a reducible header polynomial over $\mathbf F_2$. Put $q=2^m$, $F=\mathbf F_2[Z]/Q$ and $E=F[W]/R$. An irreducible factor of $R$ over $F$ of degree $b$ gives a field of order $2^{mb}$ containing its root and hence the binary field of degree $t$. Thus $t\mid mb$, and $\gcd(t,m)=1$ forces $b=t$. The next $tm$ bits give the coordinates of $z\in E$ on $Z^iW^j$ with $i$ varying fastest. Ignore the remaining $\ell=N-t-m-tm$ bits. Accept exactly when $z^e=1$ for $e=1+q+\cdots+q^{t-1}$.

The factorization of $X^{2^d}-X$ consists, once each, of all monic binary irreducibles whose degrees divide $d$, by Frobenius on finite fields. This gives an irreducibility test by divisibility into this polynomial and coprimality with $X^{2^j}-X$ for proper divisors $j$ of $d$. The tests use modular squaring and polynomial gcd. Degree-$d$ irreducibles exist since roots from proper divisors account for at most $\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}2^j<2^d$. Field operations have polynomial bit cost and $e$ has at most $tm$ bits. This defines a single polynomial-time evaluator, including on invalid headers. The NP verifier accepts iff the witness is empty and the evaluator accepts, with witness bound zero.

**Rectangles.** For any partition of the input coordinates, a one-rectangle is a Cartesian product of assignments to the two sides, all of whose concatenations accept. On one fixed valid header and tail the number of accepting payloads equals $e$: the multiplicative homomorphism $z\mapsto z^e$ on $E^*$ has image in the roots of $X^{q-1}-1$ and its kernel has at most $e$ roots. Its kernel therefore has exactly $(q^t-1)/(q-1)=e$ elements.

For distinct $d_1,\ldots,d_t\in E$, at most $t!$ elements $y$ satisfy $(y+d_i)^e=1$ for every $i$. To see this, write these as $\prod_{j=1}^t(x_j-a_{ij})=1$ with $x_j=y^{q^{j-1}}$, $a_{ij}=-d_i^{q^{j-1}}$. Each column has distinct entries and $x_1=y$. Over an algebraic closure, homogenize separately in $t$ copies of the projective line with coordinates $[u_j:v_j]$. The equations become $\prod_j(u_j-a_{ij}v_j)=\prod_jv_j$. If any $v_j=0$, each finite coordinate can kill at most one row's left product, and there are fewer than $t$ such coordinates. Hence there are no boundary solutions. The common projective zero set is finite: its projection to each projective line is closed by projectivity and misses infinity, so is finite. For the count, the Segre embedding uses as coordinates the products choosing $u_j$ or $v_j$ once for each $j$. Each equation is then a hyperplane. The Segre variety has degree $t!$: its degree-$h$ Hilbert function is $(h+1)^t$, counting the independent multihomogeneous monomials of degree $h$ in every pair. Each such monomial is a product of $h$ Segre coordinates. The leading coefficient of this Hilbert polynomial is its degree divided by $t!$. Successive hyperplane cuts cannot increase the sum of the degrees of irreducible components: retain contained components, and apply the hyperplane form of Bézout to each other component, counting multiplicities before discarding them. The final finite set has at most $t!$ points.

Partial payload assignments embed injectively in $E$, and the two parts add to $z$. A one-rectangle with $t$ rows and $t!+1$ columns contradicts the translate bound. Put $K=2^{t+m+\ell}(t!+1)$. A one-rectangle on the full input with both sides of size at least $K$ would still have at least $t!+1$ different payload assignments per side after fixing each side's header and tail by pigeonholing. Their common header is valid. Thus no such rectangle exists. Write $S$ for the set of accepted inputs. Here $t,m=\Theta(\sqrt N)$ and $0\le\ell<2(t+1)$ by the choice of $m$. Consequently $\log K=O(\sqrt N\log N)$, and $|S|\ge e\ge 2^{(t-1)m}$ gives $N-\log|S|=O(\sqrt N)$.

**Circuit lower bound.** At most $2\lceil\log K\rceil-1$ inputs can be ignored: otherwise vary two disjoint sets of $\lceil\log K\rceil$ ignored bits in an accepting assignment to get a forbidden rectangle. Take any exact circuit of size $s$ for $S$. Eventually its selected output must be a gate. Trim to output ancestors, leaving $s'\le s$ gates and $b$ used inputs. Free constants on input pins can be absorbed into the gate table, repeating the remaining feeder or a fixed input in a constant gate. Count the syntactically used primary sources $b$ after this absorption and discard newly unused sources. This charges every retained gate once, covers repeats and all sixteen truth tables, and preserves the output function.

Replace each fan-out of $h\ge1$ pins by a binary tree of $h-1$ copy vertices, recording equality of the three incident bits. The connected undirected multigraph $G$ has maximum degree three, retains both ordered gate pins and has no loops. There are $2s'$ pins fed by $s'-1+b$ sources. Thus $G$ has $s'-b+1$ copies, $V=2s'+1$ vertices and cycle rank $\nu=|E|-V+1=s'-b+1$. In particular, $r=N-b=o(N)$.

Given an ordering of width $w$, use the assignments on each edge cut as states of a layered nondeterministic read-once program. Transitions agree on persistent edges and satisfy the next vertex's table, requiring acceptance at the output. Each input vertex reads its bit, and all other layers guess. At most four assignments of incident bits obey a vertex rule, hence there are at most $4V2^w$ transitions. A path describes globally consistent wire values, so by acyclicity it accepts exactly the circuit's inputs. On the $b$ read bits, a state gives a one-rectangle of prefixes reaching it times suffixes reaching acceptance; fixing the unread bits transfers the rectangle property. Choose one accepting path for each distinct accepted assignment on the $b$ read coordinates. At each layer prefixes and suffixes are strings on disjoint coordinate sets in the same fixed read order. Charge each such assignment to the first edge across which the suffix count falls from at least $K$ to less than $K$. This edge exists eventually since the start has $|S|/2^r\ge K$ suffixes and the accept state has one. The source has fewer than $K$ prefixes, and the edge fixes the intervening bit if it reads one. Fewer than $K^2$ assignments charge an edge. It follows that $w\ge\log|S|-r-2\log K-\log(4V)$.

For completeness every connected loopless subcubic multigraph has $\operatorname{cw}(G)\le2a\nu+O(\log^2(V+1))$. In a bridgeless block contract an adjacent pair whenever one endpoint has degree at most two or the pair has parallel edges, deleting all edges internal to the pair. Bridgelessness, connectivity and the degree bound persist, and cycle rank never increases. The end is a singleton or simple cubic $H$ with $|H|=2(\nu(H)-1)$. Each contracted piece has at most three boundary edges. Expand each piece in a cubic layout by traversing its binary contraction tree, larger child first. A prefix of a partly expanded piece is covered by completed siblings on the route to its first missing leaf. After each completed sibling the route enters a child of at most half the parent's size, so there are at most $\lceil\log V\rceil$ such siblings. Their boundaries cost at most $3\lceil\log V\rceil$ above the cut immediately before the partly expanded contracted piece. The singleton case has the same bound. For general $G$, delete bridges and choose a centroid in the resulting tree of blocks weighted by vertex counts, so every hanging subproblem has at most half the vertices. Such a centroid is reached by moving into any component larger than half until none remains. Insert each hanging layout immediately before its attachment vertex of the centroid layout. Only one hanging layout is partial at a cut, and crossing attachment bridges all meet the next attachment vertex, so cost at most three. Cycle ranks add over bridges. The recursion has $O(\log(V+1))$ levels, each costing $O(\log(V+1))$ beyond the rank contributions along the active branch. This proves the bound.

If $s<cN$ then $V=O(N)$. Applying both width inequalities gives $2a(s'-b+1)\ge N-o(N)$, hence $s\ge(1+1/(2a))N-o(N)$. All losses above are uniform at every sufficiently large integer length. Since $1+1/(2a)>c$, this contradicts $s<cN$ eventually. Thus the stated family satisfies the declared eventual linear bound with zero remainder.

## Paths from single-use payload inputs

Fix a valid header and tail, let $n=tm$, and let $S\subset E$ be the accepting payloads, $H=|S|\ge2^{n-m}$. Trim a linear-size payload circuit and absorb constants and unary gates into truth tables, keeping only essential binary gates. Choose distinct single-use inputs and disjoint gate paths starting at those uses, with each nonterminal signal used only at one pin of the next gate. If these paths cover $b$ gates, then $s\ge(1+1/(2a))n+b-o(n)$, and the same holds with the number $k$ of single-use inputs in place of $b$.

For $d\ne0$ let $L=|S\cap(S+d)|$. Join $u\in dS$ to $y\in S$ when $u+y\in S$. Multiplication by $S$ acts transitively on each side, so every degree is $L$. Any $t$ distinct left vertices have at most $t!$ common neighbors, so $H\binom Lt\le t!\binom Ht$ and $L/H\le(t-1)/H+tH^{-1/t}=o(1/n)$. Thus on $1-o(1)$ of the accepts, every single bit flip rejects, excluding nonzero additive periods.

On each selected path the other pin at each gate is independent of that path's primary input by unique usage and acyclicity. A nonaffine essential binary gate is AND up to complements; on the isolated accepts its other input must take the noncanalizing value, or flipping the primary would leave the output unchanged. Enforce this value by a unary check at the feeder. The remaining path action is XOR with a fixed constant and all affine other pins. Erase the path and its primary, replacing its terminal by a new free bit with the same downstream uses, and decode the erased bit by summing the terminal, those affine feeders, and the constant. At each signal observed in a decode emit its contribution times the corresponding primary basis vector, combining all taps on a signal; untouched primaries emit their own basis coordinates. Feeds by terminals of other paths pose no problem as internal path signals have no other use. Keep acceptance and all checks. Restoring signals along paths gives a consistent accepting evaluation for every feasible decoded vector, including every isolated accept, so the possible sums $S'\subseteq S$ satisfy $\log_2|S'|\ge n-m-o(1)$.

The residual DAG has $n$ free bits and $s-b$ gates; expand fan-outs by copy trees. Its components contribute independent vector sets $A_i$ with $S'=\sum_i A_i$. Since $A+B\subseteq S$ with $|A|\ge t$ implies $|B|\le t!$, not all $|A_i|\le t!$ (otherwise the first partial sum reaching size $t$ has $\le t\,t!$ elements and the rest $\le t!$, contradicting $|S'|>t(t!)^2$). Thus some component $P$ has $|A_P|>t!$, the remaining components contribute $<t$ sums, $\log_2|A_P|\ge n-o(n)$, its $f_P$ free sources satisfy $f_P\ge\log_2|A_P|$, and its $s_P$ gates have rank $s_P-f_P+1\le s-b-n+o(n)$.

Fix an outside sum to put $A_P$ in a translate of $S$. On a width-$w$ ordering, simulate $P$ with crossing bits as states and emit vector observations on transitions in $O(|P|2^w)$ transitions. Prefix and suffix vector sets add inside this translate; charge each output to the first transition where the suffix set drops from $>t!$ to $\le t!$ vectors. Its source has $<t$ prefix sums and fixed transition label, so $\le(t-1)t!$ outputs charge it, giving $w\ge n-o(n)$ and proving the bound for $b$. For $k$ single-use inputs choose their first gates: two at the same affine gate would give a nonzero period, while at a shared nonlinear gate both inputs are fixed on all isolated accepts (at most $m+O(1)=o(n)$ coordinates). Discarding collisions leaves $k-o(n)$ distinct one-gate paths. Since $N-n=O(\sqrt N)$, the bound exceeds $4.9N$ eventually when $k\ge(7/300)n$, uniformly for $s\le5N$.
