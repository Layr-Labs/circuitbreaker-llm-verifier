# The counting theorem of Kollar-Ronyai-Szabo (supporting file for proof.md)

## 4. The counting theorem of Kollar-Ronyai-Szabo, proved self-contained

This section proves the theorem used by Sublemma NORM. The argument
follows the original proof of the norm-graph theorem [J. Kollar, L.
Ronyai, T. Szabo, "Norm-graphs and bipartite Turan numbers",
Combinatorica 16 (1996), 399-406, Theorem 3] - credited - with every
commutative-algebra step re-derived here; the fiber bound (Lemma FIB)
replaces the corresponding quoted theorem of Lang's *Algebra* by an
elementary separating-element argument.

**Theorem KRS.** Let K be a field, t >= 1, and a_ij, b_i in K for
1 <= i, j <= t, such that a_ij != a_i'j whenever i != i' (in each
column j the t entries are pairwise distinct). Then the system

    f_i(x_1, ..., x_t) := prod_{j=1}^t (x_j - a_ij) = b_i
    (i = 1, ..., t)

has at most t! solutions (x_1, ..., x_t) in K^t.

Since a solution over K is also a solution over the algebraic closure
K' of K (a field extension), it suffices to prove the bound for K
algebraically closed, which we assume from now on. Write A for the
polynomial ring K[x_1, ..., x_t], B := K[f_1, ..., f_t] for the
K-subalgebra generated by the f_i, QF(-) for fields of quotients of
domains, and

    d := [QF(A) : QF(B)]

for the degree of the field extension (finite by Lemma RANK below,
whose upper bound uses Lemma FIN). The proof has four parts. Lemma FIN
(integrality) shows A is integral over B. Lemma JAC shows the f_i are
algebraically independent over K, via the Jacobian criterion with an
explicit witness point, so B is isomorphic to a polynomial ring
K[Y_1, ..., Y_t], integrally closed in QF(B) by Lemma UFD, and m :=
(f_1, ..., f_t)·B corresponds to the ideal (Y_1, ..., Y_t), so B∖m is
multiplicatively closed and B_m is local with maximal ideal mB_m and
residue field B/m ≅ K. Lemma RANK computes d = t!. Lemma FIB (fiber
bound) then bounds every fiber of F: K^t -> K^t,
x -> (f_1(x), ..., f_t(x)) by d, so every solution set {f = b} has at
most d = t! K-points. Section 4.5 assembles this.

### 4.1 Integrality toolkit

All rings are commutative with 1; subrings share the 1. An element x of
a ring extension is *integral* over a subring S if it satisfies a
monic polynomial with coefficients in S. Zorn's lemma (classical) is
used twice below, each time for the existence of a maximal object; the
verification that chains of the considered objects have upper bounds is
given in place.

**Lemma CAY (determinant trick).** Let M be an A-module generated by
m_1, ..., m_r, and phi: M -> M an A-linear map with
phi(m_i) = sum_j c_ij m_j. Then phi satisfies its characteristic
polynomial: phi^r + c_1 phi^(r-1) + ... + c_r = 0 on M, where
T^r + c_1 T^(r-1) + ... + c_r = det(T·Id - C) for C := (c_ij).

*Proof.* Over A[T], (T·Id - C)·adj(T·Id - C) = det(T·Id - C)·Id. Both
sides are matrices over A[T]; substitute the action of phi on M for T
(scalar multiplication by T becomes application of phi, which commutes
with the A-linear maps given by the entries of adj(T·Id - C), since
these are polynomials in C with A-coefficients acting A-linearly).
The column vector m := (m_1, ..., m_r) is annihilated by the matrix
(T·Id - C) after the substitution (its i-th entry phi(m_i) -
sum_j c_ij m_j = 0); multiplying by the substituted adjugate from the
left, det(phi·Id - C)·m = 0 componentwise, i.e.
(phi^r + c_1 phi^(r-1) + ... + c_r)(m_j) = 0 for every j and every
element of M (a linear combination of the m_j). qed

**Lemma INT (integrality basics).** (a) If x_1, ..., x_n are integral
over S then S[x_1, ..., x_n] is a finite S-module. (b) If an S-algebra
R is a finite S-module, every element of R is integral over S. (c)
Transitivity (finitely generated case): if C = B[c_1, ..., c_n] is
finitely generated as a B-algebra, every element of B is integral over
A, and every c_i is integral over B, then every element of C is
integral over A. (d) The set of elements of an extension ring that
are integral over a subring S forms a subring (the *integral closure*).

*Proof.* (a) Induction: S[x_1,..,x_n] = S[x_1,..,x_{n-1}][x_n], and if
x_n is integral over S it is integral over the bigger S' :=
S[x_1,..,x_{n-1}], so S'[x_n] is spanned by 1, x_n, ..., x_n^(deg-1)
over S' (division by the monic relation), and by induction S' is
spanned by finitely many monomials over S. (b) x in R: multiplication
by x is an S-linear endomorphism of the finite S-module R; by CAY it
satisfies a monic polynomial over S. (c) By (d)
(the integral-over-A elements form a subring B-tilde containing A and,
by hypothesis, B) it suffices to show each c_i is integral over A.
Fix a monic relation c_i^m = sum_{l<m} beta_l c_i^l with beta_l in B,
and put D := A[beta_l : l < m], a finite A-module by (a) applied to
the beta_l (integral over A as elements of B). The relation shows c_i
integral over D, so D[c_i] is a finite D-module by (a), and a finite
A-module; by (b), c_i in D[c_i] is integral over A. So C ⊆ B-tilde:
every element of C is integral over A. (d) x, y integral over S:
S[x, y] is finite over S by (a), so x+y and xy, elements of S[x, y],
are integral over S by (b). qed

**Lemma LOC (localization, what we need).** For a domain R, a prime
ideal p, and S_p := R∖p (multiplicatively closed), the localization
R_p := S_p^{-1}R (fractions r/s, r in R, s in S_p, formed inside
QF(R)) is a subring of QF(R); its nonunits form the proper ideal
pR_p, so R_p is local with maximal ideal pR_p, and pR_p ∩ R = p. If R
is a finite module over a subring, R_p is a finite module over the
correspondingly localized subring.

*Proof.* Fraction arithmetic is standard. pR_p = {r/s : r in p, s in
S_p} is an ideal. A fraction r/s with r not in p is a unit (its
inverse s/r is in R_p). A fraction r/s with r in p is a nonunit: if
(r/s)(r'/s') = 1 with r' in R, s' in S_p, then r r' = s s' lies in
the ideal p, while s s' lies in S_p = R∖p: contradiction. So the
nonunits are exactly pR_p, and R_p is local. If u in R has u/1 in pR_p,
then u s = r in p for some s in S_p, and primeness of p gives u in p:
pR_p ∩ R = p. Finiteness: the image of a module generating set
generates the localized module. qed

**Lemma NAK (Nakayama).** (R, m) local, M a finite R-module, and
u_1, ..., u_p in M whose images generate M/mM. Then the u_i generate
M over R.

*Proof.* Let N be the submodule they generate; M = N + mM. If
M != N, take the finite nonzero module M/N =: M' and m' := mM'; then
M' = m M'. Choose generators w_1, ..., w_r of M' over R; writing
w_i = sum_j c_ij w_j with c_ij in m, CAY (with phi = id, which
satisfies phi(m_i) = sum c_ij m_j) gives det(Id - C)·M' = 0; but
det(Id - C) = 1 - (elements of m), a unit in the local ring R, so
M' = 0, contradiction. qed

**Lemma I2 (clearing a denominator).** A a domain, B a subring, y in A
integral over B, y != 0. Then there is z in A, z != 0, with yz in B.

*Proof.* Take a monic relation y^n + b_1 y^(n-1) + ... + b_n = 0 with
b_i in B of least degree n. If b_n = 0, then
y·(y^(n-1) + b_1 y^(n-2) + ... + b_(n-1)) = 0 and, A being a domain
with y != 0, the second factor is 0: a shorter monic relation,
contradiction. So b_n != 0 and
z := -(y^(n-1) + b_1 y^(n-2) + ... + b_(n-1)) in A satisfies
yz = b_n in B, z != 0 (else b_n = yz = 0). qed

**Lemma IFIELD (integral below a field).** Let S ⊆ R be domains with
R integral over S. If R is a field then S is a field.

*Proof.* For 0 != y in S, the inverse y^(-1) lies in the field R and
is integral over S: a monic relation
y^(-n) + s_1 y^(-(n-1)) + ... + s_n = 0 with s_i in S, multiplied by
y^(n-1), gives y^(-1) = -(s_1 + s_2 y + ... + s_n y^(n-1)) in S. So
every nonzero element of S is invertible in S. qed

**Lemma ZAR (Zariski's lemma).** Let K be an INFINITE field (for
instance an algebraically closed one: if K had q elements c_1, ...,
c_q, the polynomial 1 + prod_i (T - c_i) would have no root in K,
against algebraic closedness). A field L that is finitely generated
as a ring over K is a finite algebraic extension of K; if K is
algebraically closed, L = K.

*Proof.* Write L = K[u_1, ..., u_n]. Choose a maximal subset
{u_1, ..., u_k} of the generators that is algebraically independent
as a set (no nonzero polynomial in k variables vanishes on them; k = 0
allowed; such a subset exists since the set of sizes of independent
subsets of a finite set is nonempty and bounded). Being algebraically independent, the evaluation map
K[T_1, ..., T_k] -> K[u_1, ..., u_k], T_i -> u_i, is an isomorphism
of K-algebras (its kernel is a proper ideal and any nonzero polynomial
in it would be a dependence); we identify K[u_1, ..., u_k] with the
polynomial ring in k genuine indeterminates and its fraction field
K(u_1, ..., u_k) = F_0 with the rational function field; both embed
in L. For each j > k, the set {u_1, ..., u_k, u_j} is dependent:
some nonzero polynomial P with coefficients in K vanishes on it; read
as a polynomial in the last variable, some coefficient of P is a
nonzero polynomial in u_1, ..., u_k (independence of u_1, ..., u_k:
a nonzero coefficient polynomial cannot vanish on them), so u_j is
algebraic over F_0 (it is a root of a nonzero polynomial over F_0).

Suppose k >= 1; we derive a contradiction. Let m_j in F_0[T] be the
monic minimal polynomial of u_j over F_0 (j > k). Each
coefficient of each m_j is an element of F_0 = K(u_1, ..., u_k),
i.e. a fraction g/h with g, h in K[u_1, ..., u_k], h != 0; choose one
such representation per coefficient and let d != 0 in
K[u_1, ..., u_k] be the product of the chosen denominators h. Every
coefficient of every m_j then lies in the subring
R_0 := K[u_1, ..., u_k][1/d] of F_0: its chosen representation
satisfies h | d (h is one of the multiplied factors), so
g/h = (g·(d/h))/d with g·(d/h) in K[u_1, ..., u_k]. Hence each u_j (j > k) is integral
over R_0 (its monic m_j has coefficients in R_0), so
R := R_0[u_(k+1), ..., u_n] is integral over R_0 (INT(a), INT(c)).
Now R = L: R contains u_1, ..., u_k (they lie in R_0) and
u_(k+1), ..., u_n, hence contains K[u_1, ..., u_n] = L; and R ⊆ L
because L is a field containing R_0 and every u_j. So the field L is
integral over its subring R_0, and IFIELD makes R_0 a field.

But R_0 = K[u_1, ..., u_k][1/d] with u_1 transcendental over K is
not a field. u_1 is transcendental, so for c in K the element
u_1 - c is prime in the polynomial ring K[u_1, ..., u_k] (the
quotient by the ideal it generates is K[u_2, ..., u_k], a domain;
the ring is a UFD by Gauss's theorem, classical), and u_1 - c
divides d in K[u_1, ..., u_k] only if the remainder of d upon monic
division by u_1 - c vanishes, i.e. only if
d(c, u_2, ..., u_k) = 0 as an element of K[u_2, ..., u_k]. The
qualifying c are few: if c_1, ..., c_r are distinct and qualify, then
(u_1 - c_1)...(u_1 - c_r) divides d (pairwise coprime factors,
iterated), so r <= deg_{u_1}(d); as K is infinite, some c in K has
u_1 - c not dividing d. Fix such c. If 1/(u_1 - c) lay in R_0, say 1/(u_1 - c) = g/d^s with g in
K[u_1, ..., u_k] (an element of R_0 is a K[u_1, ..., u_k]-multiple of
1/d^s for some s), then clearing denominators inside the rational
function field
gives d^s = (u_1 - c)·g in the polynomial ring K[u_1, ..., u_k],
where u_1 - c is prime, so u_1 - c divides d^s, hence divides d
(a prime dividing a product divides a factor, iterated), contradicting
the choice of c. So 1/(u_1 - c) not in R_0 and R_0 is not a field -
contradiction.

Hence k = 0: every u_i is algebraic over K, and
L = K[u_1, ..., u_n] is a finite-dimensional K-vector space (each
adjunction of an algebraic element keeps the module finite: division
by the monic minimal polynomial), i.e. a finite algebraic extension
of K; if K is algebraically closed then every element of L, algebraic
over K, is the root of a linear polynomial over K (the minimal
polynomial over the algebraically closed K divides into linear
factors and is irreducible), so L = K. qed


**Theorem NS (weak Nullstellensatz).** K algebraically closed,
h_1, ..., h_r in K[Y_1, ..., Y_s] with no common zero in K^s. Then the
ideal (h_1, ..., h_r) is all of K[Y_1, ..., Y_s].

*Proof.* If not, extend it to a maximal ideal n (Zorn: the union of a
chain of proper ideals contains no 1 and is an upper bound). The
quotient R := K[Y_1, ..., Y_s]/n is a field, generated as a ring over K
by the classes y_j of the Y_j. The natural map K -> R is injective (its
kernel is a proper ideal of the field K), so by ZAR it is onto: R = K
as K-algebras, and there are c_j in K with y_j = c_j for all j. The
composite K[Y_1, ..., Y_s] -> R = K is a K-algebra homomorphism
sending each Y_j to c_j, i.e. it is the evaluation at
(c_1, ..., c_s); each h_i lies in n and maps to 0, so
h_i(c_1, ..., c_s) = 0 for all i: a common zero, a contradiction.
qed

### 4.2 Valuation rings

A *valuation ring* of a field M is a subring V of M such that for
every x in M, x != 0, one of x, x^(-1) belongs to V.

**Lemma V1.** A valuation ring V of M is a local domain: its nonunits
form the unique maximal ideal m_V; and V is integrally closed in M.

*Proof.* V is a domain (subring of a field). If x != 0 is a nonunit of
V and w in V, then xw is a nonunit (if xw had inverse u then
x^(-1) = w·u in V, making x a unit). For nonunits x, y with x + y != 0:
one of y/x, x/y is in V, say y/x; then x + y = x(1 + y/x) is a
nonunit times an element of V, hence a nonunit (if x + y were a unit
u, then x·(1 + y/x) = u with (1 + y/x) in V forces x to have inverse
(1 + y/x)/u in V). So the nonunits form a proper ideal m_V, and
everything outside it is a unit, so V is local with maximal ideal m_V.
Integrally closed: let x in M be integral over V; if x not in V then
x != 0 and x^(-1) in V (valuation property), and x^(-1) is a nonunit
(else its inverse would be in V, making x = (x^(-1))^(-1) in V); a
monic relation x^n + c_1 x^(n-1) + ... + c_n = 0 over V, multiplied by
x^(1-n), gives x = -(c_1 + c_2 x^(-1) + ... + c_n x^(1-n)) in V (all
powers x^(-k) are in V), a contradiction. So x in V. qed

**Lemma VZORN (domination-maximal local rings are valuation rings;
existence).** Let M be a field and C ⊆ M a subring that is local with
maximal ideal m_C. Then there is a valuation ring V of M with
C ⊆ V and m_V ∩ C = m_C (V *dominates* C).

*Proof.* Consider the local subrings C' of M dominating C (m_{C'} ∩ C
= m_C), ordered by domination (C' <= C'' iff C' ⊆ C'' and
m_{C''} ∩ C' = m_{C'}). For a chain (C_i, m_i), the union C* with
m* := ∪ m_i is an upper bound: C* is a subring, m* is a proper ideal
(1 is in no m_i), and any element of C* outside m* lies in some C_i
outside m_i, hence is a unit of C_i and of C*; so C* is local with
maximal ideal m*, and m* ∩ C_i = m_i for all i, m* ∩ C = m_C. By Zorn
fix a maximal element (A, m_A).

*Finite-extension step.* If R is a subring of M with A ⊆ R and R a
finite A-module, then R = A. Indeed, m_A R is a proper ideal of R:
otherwise R = m_A R and NAK (for the finite A-module R over the local
A) forces R = 0, absurd (1 is in R). Extend m_A R to a maximal ideal
n of R (Zorn); n ∩ A ⊇ m_A, so n ∩ A = m_A by maximality of m_A. The
localization R_n (a subring of M, LOC: elements of R∖n are nonzero
elements of the field M, so inverting them inside M realizes the
localization) is local, dominates A, and contains R; if R != A this
strictly extends (A, m_A), contradicting maximality. So R = A.

*A is integrally closed in M.* If x in M is integral over A then
A[x] is a finite A-module (INT(a)), so A[x] = A by the finite
-extension step, i.e. x in A.

*A is a valuation ring.* Let x in M, x != 0; we show x in A or
x^(-1) in A. Suppose both fail. If the ideal m_A A[x] were proper,
a maximal ideal of A[x] over m_A would make A[x]_n a local subring of
M strictly dominating A (it contains x not in A), contradicting
maximality. So m_A A[x] = A[x]: 1 = sum_{i=0}^D c_i x^i with c_i in
m_A, whence (1 - c_0) = x·(sum_{i=1}^D c_i x^(i-1)); here 1 - c_0 is a
unit of A (it lies outside m_A, else 1 = (1 - c_0) + c_0 in m_A).
Multiplying by (x^(-1))^D/(1 - c_0):

    (x^(-1))^D = (1/(1 - c_0))·sum_{i=1}^D c_i (x^(-1))^(D-i),

a monic relation over A for x^(-1). So x^(-1) is integral over A, and
A[x^(-1)] is a finite A-module, hence equals A by the finite-extension
step, giving x^(-1) in A after all - contradiction. So one of x,
x^(-1) is in A: A is a valuation ring of M dominating C. qed

**Lemma VEX (existence of separating valuation rings).** Let M be a
field, C ⊆ M a subring, C^ the integral closure of C in M, and
u in M with u not in C^. Then there is a valuation ring V of M with
C ⊆ V and u not in V.

*Proof.* u != 0 (0 is integral over any ring). Consider
D := C^[u^(-1)] ⊆ M. If u were in D, say u = sum_{i=0}^n c_i u^(-i)
with c_i in C^, then multiplying by u^n gives u^(n+1) =
sum_i c_i u^(n-i), a monic relation over C^ making u integral over the
ring C_0 := C[c_0, ..., c_n]. Every element of C_0 is integral over
C: the generators c_i lie in C^ (integral over C), so C_0 is a finite
C-module by INT(a), and INT(b) makes each of its elements integral
over C. With all elements of C_0 integral over C and u integral over
C_0, INT(c) (applied to the chain C ⊆ C_0 ⊆ C_0[u], finitely
generated) makes every element of C_0[u] integral over C, in
particular u, so u in C^: contradiction. So
u not in D, and u^(-1) is a nonunit of D (its
inverse would be u in D); let n be a maximal ideal of D containing
u^(-1) (Zorn). D_n is a local subring of M whose maximal ideal
contracts to n (LOC), so u^(-1) lies in the maximal ideal of D_n. By
VZORN there is a valuation ring V of M dominating D_n. If u were in
V, then u and u^(-1) would both lie in V with product 1, making
u^(-1) a unit of V, i.e. u^(-1) not in m_V; but
m_V ∩ D_n = maximal ideal of D_n contains u^(-1): contradiction. So
u not in V, and C ⊆ C^ ⊆ D ⊆ D_n ⊆ V. qed

The statements and proof patterns of VZORN and VEX are the standard
ones (cf. the Stacks Project, Algebra, Section 10.50, Lemmas 10.50.2,
10.50.3, 10.50.4 and 10.50.11; credited as the classical source of
this argument).

### 4.3 Finiteness (A is integral over B)

**Lemma FIN.** A is integral over B.

*Proof.* By induction on t. t = 1: f_1 = x_1 - a_11, B = K[x_1 - a_11]
= A. t >= 2: by INT(d) and VEX it suffices to show that every
valuation ring V of M := QF(A) with V ⊇ B contains x_1 (the argument
for the other x_j is identical, permuting the roles of the columns).
Suppose x_1 not in V; then, since a_i1 in K ⊆ B ⊆ V and V is a ring,
x_1 - a_i1 is not in V for every i (if x_1 - a_i1 were in V then so
would be x_1 = (x_1 - a_i1) + a_i1), so by V1,
w_i := (x_1 - a_i1)^(-1) lies in the maximal ideal m_V for every i.
Set g_j := f_j·w_j = prod_{k=2}^t (x_k - a_jk) in K[x_2, ..., x_t]
(notice j, not i: this is the j-th product with its first factor
removed); f_j = (x_1 - a_j1) g_j, so g_j = f_j·w_j lies in
B·m_V ⊆ m_V for every j.

The t-1 polynomials g_1, ..., g_(t-1) form a system of the same
product shape in the t-1 unknowns x_2, ..., x_t, with columns
2 <= k <= t distinct (a_jk != a_j'k for j != j' is given). By the
induction hypothesis K[x_2, ..., x_t] is integral over
C := K[g_1, ..., g_(t-1)]. The g_j lie in m_V ⊆ V, so C ⊆ V; since
valuation rings are integrally closed in M (V1) and x_2, ..., x_t are
integral over C ⊆ V, we get x_2, ..., x_t in V, so
K[x_2, ..., x_t] ⊆ V.

The polynomials g_1, ..., g_t (all t of them now) have no common zero
in K^(t-1): at a common zero, for every j <= t some k(j) in
{2, ..., t} has x_(k(j)) = a_(j, k(j)); with t values j and t-1
columns, two values j != j' share a column k, and then
a_(j,k) = x_k = a_(j',k) contradicts the column distinctness. By NS
there are h_j in K[x_2, ..., x_t] with sum_j h_j g_j = 1. Evaluating
in M: h_j lies in K[x_2, ..., x_t] ⊆ V and g_j in m_V, so
1 = sum h_j g_j lies in m_V, contradicting that m_V is proper. Hence
x_1 in V, and A = K[x_1, ..., x_t] ⊆ V for every valuation ring V of
M containing B. By VEX (contrapositive), every element of A is
integral over B. (A is a finitely generated B-algebra, so A is a
finite B-module by INT(a).) qed

### 4.4 The rank is t!

For a permutation sigma in S_t let I_sigma be the maximal ideal
(x_1 - a_(sigma(1) 1), ..., x_t - a_(sigma(t) t)) of A, and let
m := (f_1, ..., f_t)·B.

**Lemma IDEAL.** m·A = intersection over sigma in S_t of I_sigma.

*Proof.* Each f_i lies in every I_sigma: substituting
x_k = a_(sigma(k) k) (which defines the isomorphism A/I_sigma ≅ K)
gives f_i = prod_k (a_(sigma(k) k) - a_(i k)), and the factor with
k = sigma^(-1)(i) vanishes. So mA ⊆ ∩ I_sigma. The I_sigma are
pairwise comaximal: if sigma != tau, pick k with sigma(k) != tau(k);
then x_k - a_(sigma(k) k) is in I_sigma, x_k - a_(tau(k) k) is in
I_tau, and their difference is the nonzero constant
a_(tau(k) k) - a_(sigma(k) k) (column distinctness), so
I_sigma + I_tau = A.

For the reverse inclusion let F := f_1 f_2 ... f_t =
prod over pairs (r, k) of (x_k - a_(r k)), and for sigma in S_t let
G_sigma := F / prod_k (x_k - a_(sigma(k) k)), the product over the
t^2 - t pairs off the "graph" of sigma. Then (i) G_sigma is not in
I_sigma: modulo I_sigma it evaluates to
prod over (r,k), r != sigma(k) of (a_(sigma(k) k) - a_(r k)) != 0
by column distinctness; (ii) the f_i (i <= t) and the G_sigma
(sigma in S_t) have no common zero in K^t: a common zero of the f_i
has, for each i, some k(i) with x_(k(i)) = a_(i k(i)); the map i ->
k(i) is injective (if k(i) = k(i') then a_(i k) = x_k = a_(i' k),
so i = i'), hence a permutation sigma := the inverse of i -> k(i),
i.e. the zero is the permutation point x_k = a_(sigma(k) k); but by
(i), G_sigma does not vanish at its own permutation point. By NS there
are h_i, h'_sigma in A with

    sum_i h_i f_i + sum_sigma h'_sigma G_sigma = 1.   (*)

Let now g be in ∩_sigma I_sigma. Multiplying (*) by g:
g = sum_i (g h_i) f_i + sum_sigma (g h'_sigma) G_sigma. The first sum
lies in mA. For the second, fix sigma and write
g = sum_k (x_k - a_(sigma(k) k)) p_k (g is in I_sigma; p_k in A). Then
g·h'_sigma G_sigma = sum_k p_k h'_sigma · (x_k - a_(sigma(k) k))
·G_sigma, and (x_k - a_(sigma(k) k))·G_sigma contains among its
factors all t linear factors of f_(sigma(k)) - namely
(x_k - a_(sigma(k) k)) itself and, for each k' != k, the factor
(x_(k') - a_(sigma(k) k')) which occurs in G_sigma since
sigma(k) != sigma(k') for k' != k. These t linear polynomials are
pairwise coprime in the unique factorization domain A: each
x_(k') - a_(r k') generates a prime ideal (the quotient by it is
isomorphic to a polynomial ring in the remaining variables), two such
primes are non-associate whenever they differ, and non-associate
primes of a UFD are coprime. Their product f_(sigma(k))
therefore divides (x_k - a_(sigma(k) k))·G_sigma. So each summand lies
in the principal ideal (f_(sigma(k))) ⊆ mA, whence g in mA. qed

**Lemma JAC (the f_i are algebraically independent).** The Jacobian
matrix J := (df_i/dx_j) has determinant not identically zero; the f_i
are therefore algebraically independent over K, and B ≅
K[Y_1, ..., Y_t] (a polynomial ring; in particular a UFD, integrally
closed in QF(B) by Lemma UFD). Under this isomorphism m =
(f_1, ..., f_t)B corresponds to (Y_1, ..., Y_t), so m is a maximal
ideal, S := B∖m is multiplicatively closed, and the localization
B_m is local with maximal ideal mB_m and residue field B/m ≅ K.

*Proof.* df_i/dx_j = prod_{k != j} (x_k - a_ik). Evaluate J at the
point xi := (a_11, a_22, ..., a_tt) of K^t: entry (i, j) becomes
prod_{k != j}(a_kk - a_ik); for i = j every factor has k != j = i, so
no factor vanishes (column distinctness), while for i != j the factor
with k = i vanishes. So J(xi) is diagonal with nonzero diagonal
entries, det J does not vanish at xi, and det J != 0 as an element of
the field QF(A). Independence: suppose P(f_1, ..., f_t) = 0 for some
nonzero P in K[Y_1, ..., Y_t], chosen of least total degree. Applying
d/dx_j to the identity (the chain rule, valid for polynomials) gives,
for every j, sum_i P_i(f)·(df_i/dx_j) = 0 in A ⊆ QF(A), where
P_i := dP/dY_i. These are t linear systems over QF(A) saying that the
column vector (P_i(f))_i is annihilated by the transpose of J; since
det J != 0 in QF(A), the matrix is invertible over QF(A), so
P_i(f) = 0 in QF(A), hence in A, for every i. If some P_i were
nonzero, it would be a shorter relation, contradicting minimality; so
all P_i = 0. Then every exponent of every variable in P is divisible
by p := char K (a monomial with an exponent not divisible by p has a
nonzero partial derivative; in characteristic 0 this already forces
P constant). If deg P = 0, P is a nonzero constant and P(f) = P·1
!= 0 in the domain A: impossible. If deg P > 0: K is algebraically
closed, so every coefficient c of P has a p-th root b in K
(T^p - c has a root), and freshman's dream gives

    P(Y) = (sum_nu b_nu Y^(nu/p))^p

formally (all exponents p-divisible). So 0 = P(f) = Q(f)^p with
Q := sum b_nu Y^(nu/p) of degree deg P / p < deg P; A being a domain,
Q(f) = 0. Q is nonzero (Q^p = P != 0), so Q is a strictly shorter
nonzero relation, contradicting minimality. This contradiction proves
independence. The evaluation map
K[Y_1..Y_t] -> B, Y_i -> f_i, is then injective and onto B by
definition of B; m maps from (Y_1, ..., Y_t), so B/m ≅ K, m is
maximal (LOC applies to the prime m: B_m local with maximal ideal
mB_m). A polynomial ring over K is a UFD (Gauss's theorem,
classical). qed

**Lemma RANK.** [QF(A) : QF(B)] = t!.

*Proof.* Upper bound t!. Localize A at the multiplicatively closed set
S := B∖m (fractions a/s with a in A, s in S, formed inside QF(A);
this is a subring A_m of QF(A) containing B_m, and A_m is a finite
B_m-module because a finite A-module generating set maps to one:
A = sum B·w_l gives A_m = sum B_m·w_l). By construction
mA_m = mB_m·A_m (m is generated by the f_i, which lie in B). By IDEAL
and CRT (the I_sigma are pairwise comaximal; CRT is classical and
proved by induction from the two-ideal case, the two-ideal case via a
Bezout decomposition 1 = e + e' with e in I_1, e' in I_2),

    A/mA = A/(∩ I_sigma) ≅ ∏_sigma A/I_sigma ≅ K^(t!),

so dim_K A/mA = t!. The natural map A/mA -> A_m/mA_m is an
isomorphism. Injectivity: if a in A maps into mA_m, then
a/1 = sum_i f_i·(c_i/s_i) with c_i in A, s_i in S; putting
s := ∏ s_i (in S, multiplicatively closed) gives
a·s in mA; now s mod mA is a unit of A/mA ≅ K^(t!) - indeed s =
P(f_1, ..., f_t) with P in K[Y_1..Y_t] and P(0) != 0 (else s in m,
the preimage of (Y)), so s vanishes at no permutation point, i.e.
s mod I_sigma != 0 for every sigma, a unit of the product of fields -
and multiplying by its inverse gives a in mA. Surjectivity: for any
fraction a/s' in A_m (s' in S), the same unit argument gives s' mod mA
a unit of A/mA; choose v in A with s'·v ≡ 1 mod mA; then
(a/s') - (av)/1 = a(1 - s'v)/s' in mA_m, so a/s' is congruent to the
image of av. So dim_K A_m/mA_m = t!, and since B_m is local with
maximal ideal mB_m and residue field B_m/mB_m = B/m ≅ K (JAC), NAK
gives that the B_m-module A_m is generated by p <= t! elements
u_1, ..., u_p (lift a K-generating set of
A_m/mA_m = A_m/(mB_m A_m)).

These u_i span QF(A) over QF(B): for x/y in QF(A) with x, y in A,
y != 0, Lemma I2 applied to y (integral over B by FIN; its z lies in A
because A is a ring containing y and the polynomial expression for z)
gives z in A, z != 0, with yz in B∖{0}. Then xz in A ⊆ A_m =
sum_i B_m u_i, say xz = sum_i beta_i u_i with beta_i in B_m ⊆ QF(B),
so x/y = xz/(yz) = sum_i (beta_i/(yz))·u_i with beta_i/(yz) in
QF(B). Hence d = dim_{QF(B)} QF(A) <= p <= t!.

Lower bound t!. By JAC, B is a polynomial ring, integrally closed in
QF(B) (Lemma UFD). Lemma FIB applies with
(S, R, E, L, pi) := (B, A, QF(B), QF(A), F): every fiber of
F: K^t -> K^t has at most d points. The fiber over 0 is exactly
the set of permutation points {x_k = a_(sigma(k) k) : sigma in S_t} -
every common zero of the f_i is of this form (shown in IDEAL: the map
i -> k(i) is a bijection), each such point is a zero (the factor with
k = sigma^(-1)(i) vanishes) - and these t! points are pairwise
distinct (distinct sigma differ at some k, and columns are distinct).
So t! <= d. With the upper bound, d = t!. qed

**Lemma UFD (UFDs are integrally closed).** A unique factorization
domain R is integrally closed in QF(R): if x = a/b in lowest terms
(some common-factor-free representation, a, b != 0) satisfies a monic
x^n + c_1 x^(n-1) + ... + c_n = 0 over R, then multiplying by b^n:
a^n + c_1 a^(n-1) b + ... + c_n b^n = 0, so b divides a^n in R; every
prime factor of b divides a, and freedom from common factors makes b a
unit, x in R.

*Proof of the divisibility claim.* Let p be a prime element dividing
b. Then p divides a^n, so p divides a (a prime dividing a product
divides a factor, iterated), contradicting that a, b share no prime
factor. So b has no prime factor: in a UFD every nonzero nonunit has a
prime factor (factor it), so b is a unit. qed

### 4.5 The fiber bound and the conclusion

**Lemma FIB (fiber bound).** Let S ⊆ R be domains with R integral
over S, S integrally closed in its fraction field E := QF(S),
L := QF(R) finite over E of degree d, K an algebraically closed field,
R = K[z_1, ..., z_s] with z_1, ..., z_s GENUINE INDETERMINATES (R the
polynomial ring in them), S = K[pi_1, ..., pi_s] for polynomials
pi_i in R, and pi: K^s -> K^s, x -> (pi_1(x), ..., pi_s(x)). Then for
every b in K^s the set {x in K^s : pi_i(x) = b_i for all i} has at
most d points.

*Proof.* Let x^1, ..., x^r be any finite collection of distinct points
of the fiber (it suffices to bound every finite subcollection, so no
finiteness of the fiber is presupposed). For the v-th
point let M_v := (z_j - x^v_j : j = 1..s); this is a MAXIMAL ideal of
the polynomial ring R (the evaluation K[z_1, ..., z_s] -> K at x^v is
onto with kernel exactly M_v, the z_j being indeterminates), and it
contains pi_i - b_i for every i (pi_i - b_i vanishes at x^v). The
M_v are pairwise comaximal: M_v + M_w is an ideal containing the
maximal ideal M_v, hence equals M_v or R; it is not M_v, because
z_j - x^w_j lies in M_w and outside M_v for a coordinate j with
x^v_j != x^w_j (if it lay in M_v, its value at the point x^v, which is
x^v_j - x^w_j != 0, would have to vanish). K is infinite (if it had
q elements c_1, ..., c_q, the polynomial
1 + prod_i (T - c_i) would have no root in K, contradicting algebraic
closedness), so pick pairwise distinct c_1, ..., c_r in K; by CRT
there is c in R with c ≡ c_v mod M_v for all v. As an element of R, c
is integral over S.

*Claim: the minimal polynomial chi of c over E has coefficients in S
and degree <= d.* Let Omega ⊇ L be a splitting field of chi over E
(classical: every polynomial over a field splits over some extension
field, constructed as a quotient by an irreducible factor, iterated).
Every root theta of chi in Omega is integral over S: the evaluation
isomorphisms E[T]/(chi) ≅ E(c) and E[T]/(chi) ≅ E(theta) (chi is the
minimal polynomial of both) compose to an E-isomorphism
sigma_theta: E(c) -> E(theta) with sigma_theta(c) = theta, which
fixes the coefficients (in E) of any monic relation chi_0 over S
satisfied by c, so chi_0(theta) = sigma_theta(chi_0(c)) = 0: theta is
a root of the monic chi_0 over S, i.e. integral over S. The
coefficients of chi are, up to sign, the elementary symmetric
functions of its roots, hence integral over S by INT(d) (integral
elements of Omega over S form a subring), and they lie in E, so they
lie in the integrally closed S. Finally E(c) ⊆ L (c in R ⊆ L), so
deg chi = [E(c) : E] <= [L : E] = d by the tower law.

Write chi(T) = T^n + P_1(pi_1, ..., pi_s) T^(n-1) + ... +
P_n(pi_1, ..., pi_s) with P_l in K[Y_1, ..., Y_s] (each coefficient
is an element of S = K[pi_1, .., pi_s]). Evaluate chi(c) = 0 in R and
reduce modulo M_v: the coefficient P_l(pi) reduces to
P_l(pi(x^v)) = P_l(b_1, ..., b_s) - the same for every v - and c
reduces to c_v. So

    chibar(T) := T^n + P_1(b) T^(n-1) + ... + P_n(b) in K[T],

a monic polynomial of degree n <= d, vanishes at each of the r
pairwise distinct elements c_v of K, whence r <= n <= d (LINPOLY).
qed

**Conclusion of Theorem KRS.** By FIN, A is integral over B; by RANK
and its proof, B is a polynomial ring, integrally closed in QF(B), and
d = t!; by FIB, every set {x in K^t : f_i(x) = b_i ∀ i} has at most
d = t! points. This is Theorem KRS. qed

*A remark on sources.* The reduction to integrality, the rank
computation mA = ∩ I_sigma with Nakayama, and the overall architecture
are from the original proof of the norm-graph theorem (Kollar-Ronyai-
Szabo 1996, Theorem 3 and its Lemma), which quotes a fiber bound from
Lang's *Algebra* (Ch. II, Sec. 6.3, Thm 3); our Lemma FIB replaces that
quote with the separating-element argument above, and the valuation
-existence steps (VZORN, VEX) re-derive the standard commutative
algebra (cf. Stacks Project 10.50.2-10.50.11) in full.
