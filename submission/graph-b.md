## Evidence B1: fair local cubic cuts

# Universal fair local cubic cuts by simultaneous downward patch minimization

**Intermediate computer-assisted theorem.** There is a fixed finite radius and a scalar Bernoulli rule for every simple cubic graph H with a perfect matching (the base rule may depend on H). Its physical vertex bits each have marginal exactly one half, depend only on independent marks in the bounded neighborhood of that vertex, and the expected number of actual crossing edges is at most **0.124617 |V(H)|**. Consequently H has a bisection with at most (0.124617+o(1))|V(H)| crossing edges, uniformly in H. For any bounded deterministic weights the signed imbalance is o(n) simultaneously with the cut bound by concentration. This is already strictly below 1/8. This packet itself proves one fair cut, not an all-prefix cutwidth of 1/8. Applied under the separate physical balanced-separator assembly and Li kernel counting hypotheses, 1+1/(2c)=624617/124617 >5.01229 for the rounded c. ## 1. A globally compatible Gaussian source, including branched neighborhoods

Contract the perfect matching to anchors of a loopless 4-regular multigraph Q. Its four labeled incidences are paired into two straight continuations, one physical vertex per pair. Its marked universal tree has two lines through every anchor. Set t=41/57, a=28/57, delta=t-a, so t^2+2a^2=1. We need the joint law on whole finite **branching balls**, not just agreement of covariances on two central lines.

Give anchors independent standard Gaussians Z. On an incoming oriented edge uv (source u), let B^0_uv=Z_u. For each further shell j+1, take t times the depth-j message arriving from the straight opposite direction at u plus a times each from the other two directions. Each B^j_uv has variance one by disjointness of its three predecessor half-trees. Different shells of that one oriented edge use disjoint distance layers. Set B^[J]_uv to the sum of shells 0,...,J-1 divided by sqrt(J); it has unit variance. The four incoming variables at a given root are iid standard normals, and any collections of inward-oriented descendant rows on disjoint forward half-trees are independent. All these finite rules are equivariant and local. In mean square the discrepancy of B^[J] from the t,a,a recurrence applied to its neighbors' B^[J] has norm sqrt(2/J), since only the first and next omitted shells survive. Along a diagonal subsequential finite-dimensional Gaussian limit the recurrence is exact and that half-tree independence persists. Equivalently each finite-dimensional covariance family is bounded and successively compact; the tree is countable. The resulting local laws are unique by the following recursive sampler.

Define the root anchor score R as the half-sum of its four incoming B modes. For any chosen forward direction with incoming mode B, the three modes at its source farther into this branch are independent normals before conditioning, constrained by B=t B_f+a(B_l+B_r). Conditional on B, they may be generated by two independent new standard normals Z,Y as follows: B_f=tB+sqrt(2)a Z, W_0=sqrt(2)a B-t Z, B_l=(W_0+Y)/sqrt(2), B_r=(W_0-Y)/sqrt(2). The backward mode into this site is determined by the earlier three incident modes, by the same t,a,a recurrence. Different new subtrees are conditionally independent given their entering modes: their full inward families were independent across disjoint half-trees, and the value conditioned on for each half is measurable there. Previously revealed upstream modes involve no additional data from these halves beyond those entering modes, again by the recurrence. This inductively determines the joint law on each finite tree.

Here is the two-real-state version used by the certificate; all transition coefficients are rational. Generate independent standard R,U shared by the two lines. Draw an independent V~N(0,1/2) for each line ell=+1,-1. For each initial ray on that line, set

## 3. Simultaneous local erasure cannot increase the same edge-split energy

On a finite graph let S be the set of positive edges of the initial assignment and E(S)=sum_v NAE on its incident bits. E is a submodular set function (each NAE is submodular). For a patch B of allowed edge variables choose the least minimizer X_B among subsets of S agreeing with S outside B. It exists by meet/join closure. Its removal set is C_B=S minus X_B. For any union D of previously selected removals, submodularity gives
```
E(S minus (D union C_B)) <= E(S minus D)+E(S minus C_B)-E(S minus (D intersection C_B)).
```
The last set is feasible on the B patch: it differs from S only by deletions inside B. Thus the last two costs have nonpositive difference. Induction proves that the sum of split indicators cannot increase for **any simultaneously activated subcollection**, with arbitrary overlap. All patches are minimized against the same original S, not sequentially recomputed. This is a claim on edge-bit split energy, not an assertion that two unrelated vertex mincuts can be combined.

Take the Q-ball of radius 40 at every anchor. Its patch consists of all matching edges of anchors in that ball together with C edges joining two of its anchors. Fix all other edge bits. On the universal tree these are identical finite shapes. Activate a patch iff its own independent uniform mark <=lambda. Intersect S with the local minimizing assignments of all activated patches. Physical vertex bits are the majority of the resulting three edge bits. For every finite radius the policy is equivariant, and all its operations, including decisions at other centers that can delete an incident edge, are bounded-radius. The means of its split indicators on the marked tree do not exceed the initial means: realize any specified finite neighborhoods on a finite marked 4-regular graph of sufficiently high girth, apply the deterministic energy inequality there, and use equality of the independent-mark local statistics. Such graphs exist elementarily: on the reduced words in two generators of length at most L, the two partial right translations can each be extended to a permutation of that finite set. The finite group they generate has no reduced nontrivial relation of length <=L (evaluate at the empty word). Its Cayley graph supplies the two marks, and opening each anchor into two physical vertices joined by M recovers a simple cubic graph for large L. This use of high girth proves a statement on the source *tree*; the ultimate target graph need not have large girth.

## 4. Rigorous upper bound on positive majority after all patches activate

For a root edge or vertex, deleting under all active patches can only lower its bits from the center patch solution. That center patch still fixes every originally negative edge and has actual exterior boundary bits <=1. Relax it by **keeping only raw-negative matching pins** (these are truly zero by the one-sided initial repair), dropping all other interior upper bounds, and setting its C-boundary to all ones. Least minimizers can only increase: use submodularity for the two solutions, their meet feasible under the smaller upper bounds and smaller boundary, and join feasible under the larger constraints. Treat boundary as fixed variables in both comparisons. Consequently the relaxed least root majority bounds the fully patched physical majority from above. This does not claim that the relaxed assignment is the actual result of the patches.

Compute that relaxed tree solution exactly. A directed subtree reports its zero-cost minus one-cost difference on the incident edge to its parent, clamped to {-1,0,1}. At a cubic NAE vertex with two incoming messages x,y the outgoing difference is sign(x+y) after clamping the two incoming differences; this follows directly by minimizing four cases, equivalently paying NAE on the two child bits and parent bit. When a boundary edge is fixed 1 or 0 the corresponding infinite difference may safely be clamped: changing that bit affects at most one adjacent NAE by one, hence all parent fixed-bit minimum-value differences are unchanged. Combining the two physical endpoints at a matching anchor, a site with straight incoming x and perpendicular pair y,z sends
```
sign(x-1) if raw X<=T,          sign(x+sign(y+z)) otherwise.
```
