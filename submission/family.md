# Norm-one membership, the circuit transfer and scalar covariance bounds

## 1. The language

Reject at lengths `N<16`. Otherwise put

```
k = max{j : 4^j <= N},   t = 2^k,
m = largest odd integer <= floor((N-t)/(t+1)),
ell = N-t-m-tm.
```

Read `t` coefficients of a monic degree-`t` polynomial `R(W)` over
`F_2`, then `m` coefficients of a monic degree-`m` polynomial `Q(Z)`
over `F_2`. Reject if `m<1` or either polynomial is reducible. A monic
degree-`d` polynomial `P` is irreducible exactly when it divides
`X^(2^d)-X` and is coprime to `X^(2^j)-X` for every proper positive
divisor `j` of `d`. Indeed an irreducible of degree `a` divides the
squarefree `X^(2^j)-X` exactly when `a|j`, by the order of Frobenius
on its field. This proves the test. It also gives `2^d=sum_(a|d) a I_a`
for the counts `I_a` of monic irreducibles. The contribution of proper
divisors is bounded by `sum_(a<=d/2) 2^a < 2^d`, so valid headers exist.

Set `F=F_2[Z]/Q`, `q=2^m`, and `E=F[W]/R`. An irreducible factor of
`R` of degree `a` over `F` gives a field of size `2^(ma)` containing
the degree-`t` field generated by a root of `R`. Hence `t|ma`. The
power of two `t` is coprime to the odd `m`, so `a=t` and `E` is a field.
The next `tm` bits encode `z=sum_(u<m,v<t) c_uv Z^u W^v` with `u`
varying fastest. Ignore the final `ell` bits. Accept exactly when
`z^e=1`, for `e=1+q+...+q^(t-1)`. The encoding of `z` is an additive
bijection. Polynomial GCD and modular squaring check the prefixes in
polynomial time. Arithmetic modulo the two monic polynomials has
polynomial bit cost and `e` has at most `N` bits. One verifier accepts
exactly the empty witness on every accepting input of this evaluator.

## 2. Many accepts and small rectangles

Fix a valid header and the unused tail to zero and call the resulting
payload function `g`. The image of the homomorphism `z -> z^e` on
`E*` lies among the at most `q-1` roots of `X^(q-1)-1`. Its kernel
has at least `e` points and the root bound gives at most `e`. Write
`N1(g)=e>=2^((t-1)m)` for the accepting count.

For `t` distinct shifts `d_i` in `E`, the simultaneous equations
`(y+d_i)^e=1` give `prod_j(x_j-a_ij)=1`, with
`x_j=y^(q^(j-1))` and `a_ij=-d_i^(q^(j-1))`. Frobenius is injective,
so the shifts in each column are distinct. The product-equation bound
proved below gives at most `t!` solutions over `E`. Since `x_1=y`,
at most `t!` elements satisfy all the translates.

Each payload partition encodes `z` as a sum of two injectively encoded partial
assignments. An all-one rectangle with `t` rows and `t!+1` columns
would supply the forbidden translates. Denote this property by
`D(t,t!+1)`, including partitions with an empty side.

Let `S_N` be the accepting set on all N bits. Set `K_N=1` for
N<16 and `K_N=2^(t+m+ell)(t!+1)` otherwise. If an all-one rectangle in `S_N` has both
sides of size at least `K_N`, separately fix the header and tail
coordinates on each side by pigeonholing. At least `t!+1`
assignments survive on each side. They specify one valid header,
since they form a rectangle of accepting inputs. Projection onto
the payload is injective on each side after this fixing. The resulting
payload rectangle contradicts `D(t,t!+1)` for that header.

The parameter formulas give `sqrt(N)/2<t<=sqrt(N)`,
`sqrt(N)-4<m<2sqrt(N)` and `0<=ell<=2t+1`. Thus
`log K_N=O(sqrt(N) log N)=o(N)`. One valid header already gives
`|S_N|>=2^((t-1)m)`, so `N log 2-log |S_N|=O(sqrt(N))=o(N)`.

### Product-equation lemma

Over a field \(K\), let \(a_{1j},\ldots,a_{tj}\) be distinct for
each \(j\). For \(t\ge1\) and \(b_1,\ldots,b_t\in K\), the system

\[
\prod_{j=1}^t (x_j-a_{ij})=b_i\qquad (1\le i\le t)
\]

has at most \(t!\) solutions. [Kollár, Rónyai
and Szabó, Theorem 3.3](https://web.vu.lt/mif/s.jukna/boolean/Kollar-norm-graphs.pdf).

Work over an algebraic closure \(k\) of \(K\). Give the \(j\)-th
projective line coordinates \([u_j:v_j]\). On \(X=(\mathbb P^1_k)^t\)
the homogenized equations are

\[
F_i = \prod_j(u_j-a_{ij}v_j)-b_i\prod_jv_j=0.
\]

If a point has some \(v_j=0\), then the second product vanishes and
each infinite coordinate contributes a nonzero factor to every first
product. Each finite coordinate can kill the first product in at most
one row because its shifts are distinct. There are fewer than \(t\)
finite coordinates, so at least one row remains nonzero. Consequently
the common zero scheme \(Z\) lies in the single affine chart
\(\prod_j v_j\ne0\). It is closed in that chart and in \(X\).
Its structural morphism is affine and proper, hence finite.
[Stacks Project, Lemma 29.45.11](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01WN).

Write \(L=\mathcal O_X(1,\ldots,1)\). The equations are \(t\) sections
of \(L\). At each point of \(Z\), their local equations form a system
of parameters in the regular local ring of the smooth \(t\)-fold
\(X\), since \(Z\) is finite. They form a regular sequence by
Cohen–Macaulayness. Away from \(Z\), one local equation is a unit.
Thus the Koszul complex resolves \(\mathcal O_Z\), with term
\(L^{-i}\) repeated \(\binom ti\) times in degree \(i\).
[Systems of parameters](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02JN) ·
[Koszul exactness](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/062F).

Twist the resolution by \(L^m\) for \(m\ge t\) and take Euler
characteristics. On each projective line the degree \(m-i\) bundle
has \(m-i+1\) global sections and vanishing higher cohomology.
The product formula gives \(\chi(X,L^{m-i})=(m-i+1)^t\).
An invertible sheaf on a finite scheme has one copy of each local
Artin ring as its local module, so \(\chi(Z,L^m|_Z)\) is the length
of \(Z\). Therefore this length is

\[
\sum_{i=0}^t(-1)^i\binom ti(m-i+1)^t=t!,
\]

the \(t\)-th backward difference of a monic polynomial of degree \(t\).
Every geometric point contributes at least one to the length, so the
same upper bound holds for the original \(K\)-points.
[Projective-space cohomology](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01XS) ·
[Cohomology of products](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BEC).

## 3. Circuit transfer with actual lengths and zero remainder

Use base-two logarithms and the circuit/program route of McGuire,
Schlesinger and Williams (2026). Put `a_N=N-log|S_N|`, `q_N=ceil(log K_N)`
and `r_N=2q_N-1`. By §2 these are `o(N)` and `|S_N|>0` eventually.

### Used inputs

If a nonempty Boolean set with D(K,K), K>=2, ignores a set of r
coordinates, then r<=2ceil(log K)-1. Otherwise take two disjoint ignored
subsets of size ceil(log K), vary them freely on opposite sides of a
partition and keep any accepting assignment fixed elsewhere. This gives
a forbidden rectangle. Applied to S_N, this proves that any circuit
computing it uses at least N-r_N input variables. It also excludes a
constant or input-projection output for all sufficiently large N.

### Read-once programs

A layered nondeterministic program has singleton start and accept layers
and edges only to the next layer. Guess layers have unlabelled edges.
Each read layer labels edges by bits of one variable, read in that layer
alone. Accept iff some start-to-accept path agrees with the input.
Size counts edges with multiplicity.

Suppose such a program computes a nonempty S with D(K,K), leaves r
variables unread, and |S|>=2^r K. Then

    |P|>=|S|/[2^r(K-1)^2].                                (3)

Delete the unread coordinates from the domain, obtaining S' with
|S'|=|S|/2^r and the same D(K,K): a forbidden rectangle for S' extends
to one for S by placing unread variables on one side. At a node v let
A_v be the read prefixes reaching v and B_v the suffixes reaching accept.
A_v times B_v is an all-one rectangle. Hence |B_v|>=K implies
|A_v|<=K-1. At start |B_v|=|S'|>=K; at accept |B_v|=1<K.
For every accepting assignment choose a path and charge it to the first
edge u->v with |B_v|<K. A prefix in A_u, a suffix in B_v and the edge's
label if present reconstruct the assignment. At most (K-1)^2 assignments
charge any edge, proving (3).

### Wire graphs of full-B2 circuits

An N-input size-s circuit has one selected output and s acyclic gates,
each with two ordered pins and any of the sixteen binary truth tables.
Count constant-table, projection, negation and unary gates once each.
Allow unrestricted sharing, fan-out, depth and coincident pins. Inputs,
wires and output selection cost zero. Even free constant nodes cause
no loss. Absorb a constant pin into its table and feed it by the other
feeder. With two constant pins use a constant table fed twice by x_1.
Delete gates outside the output's ancestors. The used-input bound
excludes constant and input outputs for large N. Thus the remaining
s'<=s gates have a gate output of fan-out zero and every other gate has
at least one outgoing pin.

Let n_u be its number of used inputs and t(v) the number of pins fed by
node v, counting repeated pins separately. For every t(v)>=1 distribute
v's outgoing value through a binary tree with t(v) terminal pin edges
and t(v)-1 new copy vertices of degree three, with root edge at v.
When t(v)=1 use a direct pin edge. Each copy imposes equality of its
three incident edge bits. Terminal edges meet their destination gates,
retaining pin order. The result G is a connected loopless multigraph:
following ancestor paths leads to the output, and an acyclic gate
cannot feed itself. Gate vertices have their two pin edges and, except
at the output, one outgoing root edge. Used inputs have degree one,
the output degree two, and other gates/copies degree three.

There are n_u+s'-1 nodes with outgoing pins and sum_v t(v)=2s'. Thus

    copies=s'-n_u+1, V=2s'+1, E=3s'-n_u+1,
    nu(G)=E-V+1=s'-n_u+1.                                (4)

Take any ordering of G with width w, where a cut counts all edges with
one endpoint in its vertex prefix, including parallel multiplicities.
At every cut make a program state for every assignment to its edges.
At the next vertex, consecutive states must agree on common cut edges
and satisfy its local rule: a gate's outgoing bit is its table applied
to its ordered pins; at the output that table must equal one; a copy
has all three bits equal. An input layer reads its variable and labels
each edge by the value of its unique incident graph edge. Other layers
guess. Start and accept cuts are empty and give singleton layers.

The union of two consecutive cuts is their intersection disjoint-union
the current vertex's incident edges. At most four assignments satisfy
any gate's local rule, including the output; at most two satisfy a copy
or input rule. Hence each layer has at most 4*2^w edges, and

    |P|<=4V*2^w.                                         (5)

Every graph edge persists through consecutive cuts, so a path specifies
one global edge assignment; conversely such an assignment specifies a
path. Induction in the original circuit's topological order forces the
input values, distributor equalities and every gate evaluation. The
output rule accepts exactly S_N. Every used input is read once; exactly
the other r=N-n_u inputs are unread. Since r<=r_N=o(N), §2 gives
|S_N|>=2^r K_N eventually, uniformly over circuits. Combining (3),(5)
with an optimal graph ordering yields

    cw(G)>=log|S_N|-r-2log(K_N-1)-2-log V.                (6)

### Simple cubic to subcubic layouts

Put g=500000000/3827670601. Choose 0<a<g above the limit in `proof.md`
and absorb smaller orders in C>=0. Then every connected simple cubic H
has cw(H)<=a|H|+C. For D=C+9, we prove for every connected loopless
subcubic G that

    cw(G)<=2a nu(G)+D ceil(log(V+1))^2.                   (7)

First let B be bridgeless. Repeatedly contract adjacent p,q, deleting
all r internal parallel edges, whenever one endpoint has degree at
most two or r>=2. Its degree is deg(p)+deg(q)-2r<=3 and
edges-minus-vertices decreases by r-1. This preserves connectivity,
looplessness and bridgelessness: an alternate path avoiding a surviving
edge projects to a walk avoiding it. Termination leaves either one
vertex or a connected simple cubic H, with

    |H|/2=E(H)-V(H)<=E(B)-V(B)=nu(B)-1.                   (8)

Record a binary contraction tree for each terminal piece, with
its original vertices as leaves. Every contracted piece has original edge
boundary at most three, its degree when created. Order a piece's leaves
depth first, with children in nonincreasing order of leaf count. In a
proper prefix, earlier siblings along the path to the first missing
leaf give at most one piece per level. Whenever an earlier sibling
exists, the chosen child has at most half the parent's leaves. There
are at most ceil(log V) such levels, hence at most 6ceil(log V) edges
leave those earlier pieces. Expanding each H vertex in any specified
H order therefore adds at most 6ceil(log V) to its width: whole pieces
form an H-prefix and only one further piece is partial. The one-vertex
termination case has that same logarithmic width directly. Consequently
every bridgeless block B of size at most V has width at most

    2a nu(B)+6ceil(log V)+C.                             (9)

For general connected loopless G, delete its bridges. Their remaining
components are bridgeless blocks, including singleton vertices. Every
component F of G-V(B) joins a chosen block B by exactly one bridge:
two attachments would create a cycle through a purported bridge.
Counting edges and vertices gives nu(G)=nu(B)+sum_F nu(F).
Given orders of B and its hanging components, insert each F's entire
order just before its attachment vertex in B. A cut has at most the
width of B, the width of one partially listed F, and three attachment
bridges: any crossing attachment meets the next unlisted B vertex.
Thus its width is at most cw(B)+max_F cw(F)+3.

Choose B so every F has at most half G's vertices. Such a block exists:
move across a bridge into any component larger than half; at the new
block the component containing the old block is smaller than half, and
any further large component is a proper subset of the previous one.
The process terminates. Set K_V=6ceil(log V)+C+3. Induction on n<=V,
using that centroid block at each step, gives width at most

    2a nu(G')+(floor(log n)+1)K_V.

Indeed the hanging components have size <=n/2, cycle ranks are
nonnegative and additive, and (9) plus three bridges costs K_V.
Singletons and blocks without attachments obey the same bound. Finally
L=ceil(log(V+1))=floor(log V)+1 satisfies
L K_V<=L(6L+C+3)<=(C+9)L^2, proving (7).

### Every sufficiently large integer length

Set c0=4827670601/1000000000=1+1/(2g). Suppose a circuit for S_N has
s< s0=ceil(c0 N); larger sizes already satisfy the target. Put
V0=2s0+1 and L0=ceil(log(V0+1)). Since V=2s'+1<=V0, (4),(6),(7) give

    s>=N-r_N-1
       +[N-a_N-r_N-2log(K_N-1)-2-log V0-D L0^2]/(2a)
      =(1+1/(2a))N-R_N,
    R_N=r_N+1+[a_N+r_N+2log(K_N-1)+2+log V0+D L0^2]/(2a). (10)

By §2 and V0=O(N), R_N=o(N) over every integer length. The constants
a,C,D are fixed before N and the circuit.
Since a<g, the positive gap 1/(2a)-1/(2g) absorbs R_N for every
sufficiently large integer N. Thus every full-B2 circuit satisfies

    C_B2(L_N)>=(4827670601/1000000000)N

at all such lengths, with zero subtracted remainder and an unspecified
threshold. Small lengths keep the same total language and verifier.
