# One uniform norm-one language and its full-B2 graph bridge

This file proves the circuit/family implication used by the graph theorem.
Its sole graph input is H(gamma,h): gamma>0, h is nonnegative and
nondecreasing with h(D)/D tending to zero, and every connected cubic
multigraph on D vertices has cutwidth at most gamma D+h(D). Loops count
twice toward degree and never cross a cut; parallel edges count separately.
The graph proof supplies this input uniformly over finite graphs. No
judgment on another submission is a premise.

The result below is for one language L, at every sufficiently large
integer actual input length N, in the full sixteen-table B2 model:

    C_B2(L_N) >= (1+1/(2gamma))N-o(N).                 (B1)

The o(N) is uniform in N, rather than restricted to a subsequence. The
explicit remainder is given in Section 6. Consequently any fixed rational
c<1+1/(2gamma) satisfies C_B2(L_N)>=cN at every sufficiently large integer
N, with eventual subtracted remainder zero. The threshold is unspecified.

Credit: the circuit-to-wire-graph, read-once-program and rectangle route is
due to S. McGuire, S. Schlesinger and R. Williams, *Explicit functions
requiring larger than 4.5n size circuits* (2026). The norm-graph algebra is
Kollár--Rónyai--Szabó, *Norm-graphs and bipartite Turán numbers*,
Combinatorica 16 (1996), 399--406. The validated polynomial-prefix device
was used in earlier CircuitBreaker work crediting mooselumph. Every
nonstandard implication used here, including the KRS bound, is proved below.
We use elementary field theory, polynomial factorization/root counting,
finite-dimensional linear algebra, and the textbook commutative-algebra
facts stated explicitly in Section 7.

## 1. The language on every input

For N>=16 set

    k=max{j:4^j<=N}, t=2^k, m0=floor((N-t)/(t+1)),
    m=m0 if m0 is odd, and m=m0-1 otherwise,
    ell=N-t-m-tm.

The input consists, in order, of t coefficients for a monic polynomial
R(W) of degree t over F_2, m coefficients for a monic polynomial Q(Z) of
degree m, tm payload bits c_uv (0<=u<m, 0<=v<t, with u varying fastest),
and ell unused bits. Thus N=t+m+tm+ell exactly. On N<16 the evaluator
returns zero. On other inputs it rejects if m<1 or either prefix fails
the following irreducibility test for a monic degree-d polynomial P:

    X^(2^d)=X modulo P, and
    gcd(P,X^(2^j)-X)=1 for every proper positive divisor j of d.

All modular powers are computed by repeated squaring. If both tests pass,
form F=F_2[Z]/(Q), E=F[W]/(R), q=2^m, and

    z=sum_(v<t) sum_(u<m) c_uv Z^u W^v,
    e=1+q+...+q^(t-1).

Return one exactly when z^e=1. This defines a single total language L;
prefixes are checked input bits, never advice. Only the N original input
bits are circuit inputs. A verifier accepts precisely the empty witness
when this evaluator accepts, with witness bound zero.

Here and below elementary polynomial and finite-field facts are over
arbitrary characteristic unless F_2 is specified.

**Field and test facts.** If P is irreducible of degree d over F_2, its
quotient field has 2^d elements. Frobenius sigma:a->a^2 is an automorphism
of order exactly d: sigma^d is the identity by the finite multiplicative
group, and if sigma^j is the identity then all 2^d elements are roots of
X^(2^j)-X, forcing j>=d. Division of j by d then proves

    P divides X^(2^j)-X iff d divides j.              (B2)

The polynomial X^(2^d)-X has derivative one, hence is squarefree. Its
irreducible factors are, by (B2), precisely those whose degrees divide d.
Thus the displayed test accepts every irreducible and rejects any reducible:
a proper irreducible factor of degree j|d violates its gcd test. If I_j
counts monic degree-j irreducibles, comparison of degrees gives
2^d=sum_(j|d)j I_j. Since j I_j<=2^j and every proper divisor of d is at
most floor(d/2), d I_d>=2^d-sum_(1<=j<=floor(d/2))2^j>0 (also directly
for d=1,2). Therefore valid prefixes exist at every required degree.

Since t is a power of two and m is odd, gcd(t,m)=1. To see that R stays
irreducible over F, take a degree-d irreducible factor over F. Its quotient
field has 2^(md) elements and contains a root omega of R. The F_2-algebra
generated by omega is the degree-t field, so t divides md, hence t divides
d. As d<=t, d=t. Therefore E is a field of size q^t. The tm monomials
Z^uW^v form an F_2-basis; payload encoding z is a bijection and is additive
under bitwise XOR.

All arithmetic is uniform and polynomial time. Testing a degree-d prefix
takes O(d^4) bit operations by at most d divisors, d modular squarings per
test and polynomial gcd. Multiplication in E takes O(t^2m^2)<=O(N^2)
bit operations using polynomial multiplication and reduction by monic R,Q.
The exponent e has (t-1)m+1<=N bits, so exponentiation uses O(N) products.
The complete evaluator is O(N^4). Thus L is in P and the empty-witness NP
verifier proves both directions of membership at every length.

## 2. Payload density and the rectangle property

For g on a Boolean variable set Y, write N1(g)=|g^(-1)(1)|. Say g has
D(K1,K2) if, for every partition Y=S disjoint-union T (empty sides allowed),
no all-one rectangle A times B has |A|>=K1 and |B|>=K2.

Fix any valid prefix pair and fix unused tail bits to zero; the restricted
payload function is g(c)=[z(c)^e=1]. The map z->z^e on E* has image of
size at most q-1, since its image consists of roots of X^(q-1)-1. The
group homomorphism theorem gives kernel size at least e, while the degree-e
root bound gives at most e. Consequently

    N1(g)=e>=q^(t-1)=2^(m(t-1)).                     (B3)

Section 7 proves the KRS algebraic bound: if a_ij are distinct as the
equation index j varies, separately for each variable index i, then

    product_(i=1)^t (x_i-a_ij)=b_j, j=1,...,t

has at most t! solutions over any field. Apply it over E to t distinct
d_j, with a_ij=d_j^(q^(i-1)), b_j=1 and x_i=y^(q^(i-1)). Frobenius is
injective, so the shifts satisfy its hypotheses; the first coordinate
recovers y. Therefore at most t! values of y obey (d_j+y)^e=1 for all j.

For a payload partition S,T, define iota_S(alpha)=z(alpha union 0_T)
and iota_T(beta)=z(0_S union beta). They are injective and
z(alpha union beta)=iota_S(alpha)+iota_T(beta). An all-one rectangle with
t distinct alpha and t!+1 distinct beta would contradict the preceding
translate bound. Thus

    g has D(t,t!+1).                                (B4)

If a nonzero g with D(K1,K2) ignores a set U of variables, then

    |U|<=ceil(log2 K1)+ceil(log2 K2)-1.               (B5)

Otherwise split off disjoint subsets U1,U2 of U of the two ceiling sizes.
Starting from any accepting assignment, vary U1 freely on one side of a
partition and U2 freely on the other. Independence from U creates a
forbidden all-one rectangle. This also covers constant or projection
restrictions when their number of ignored payload variables is too large.

## 3. The exact program loss

A program has layers V_0,...,V_lambda, singleton start/accept layers, and
edges only between consecutive layers. A layer either guesses (unlabelled
edges) or reads one variable (each edge labelled zero or one). Different
read layers read different variables. An input is accepted iff some
start-to-accept path has all its read labels equal to that input. Size is
the number of edges; parallel edges are distinct. No determinism,
unambiguity or width restriction is needed.

Fixing input variables in such a program deletes incompatible edges in
their read layers and makes the surviving layers guesses. It preserves
the restricted function and cannot increase size; read layers of other
variables are unchanged.

Suppose a program computes g with D(K1,K2), leaves r variables unread,
and N1(g)>=2^r K2. Then

    |P|>=N1(g)/[2^r(K1-1)(K2-1)].                   (B6)

To prove this, delete unread coordinates from the variable set, obtaining
g' with N1(g')=N1(g)/2^r and the same D property (extend one rectangle
side by all unread assignments). At a node v in layer i, let P_v be the
prefix assignments reaching v and F_v the suffix assignments from v to
accept. They involve disjoint sets of variables, so P_v times F_v is
all-one. Hence |F_v|>=K2 implies |P_v|<=K1-1. At start, |F_start|>=K2;
at accept, |F_accept|<=1<K2. For each accepting input choose one path and
charge it to its first edge u->v with |F_v|<K2. Its prefix before that
edge is in P_u and its suffix after it in F_v. Any read variable on the
edge has the edge's fixed label. Thus at most (K1-1)(K2-1) accepting
inputs charge any edge, proving (B6). The argument works for guess layers
and for the zero-layer convention as well (the latter cannot meet its
assumed accepting-count inequality).

## 4. All sixteen B2 tables, the wire graph and its sweep

A size-s circuit has N inputs, one selected output and s acyclic gates,
each with two ordered pins and any binary Boolean truth table. Fan-out,
depth, sharing and coincident pins are unrestricted. Each gate costs one,
including constant tables, projections and negated projections. Inputs,
wires and output selection cost zero. Even allowing free constant nodes
does not weaken the following reduction.

For N>=1, absorb a constant pin into its gate's truth table and feed that
pin by the other feeder again; with two constant pins, retain a
constant-table gate reading x_1 twice. This preserves gate count and
values. Remove all nonancestors of the selected output. Unless the function
is constant or a projection, the output is a gate, has fan-out zero, and
every other retained gate has at least one outgoing pin. Thus the normal
form has s'<=s gates, no constant nodes, and only output ancestors. Unary
and constant-table gates remain counted; negation is already a binary
table with a dummy pin. No circuit input is fixed in this normalization.

For each used node v, let t(v)>=1 be the number of gate pins it feeds,
counting both pins separately even if they belong to the same gate. Route
its outgoing signal through a binary distributor: a rooted chain of t(v)-1
new degree-three copy vertices, t(v) terminal pin edges, and one root edge
at v; for t(v)=1 use just its direct pin edge. Each copy's three edges must
carry the same bit. Identify terminal ends with their gate vertices.
This yields a connected loopless multigraph G. Gate vertices have their
two distinct ordered pin edges and, except at the output, one root edge.
Used inputs have degree one; the output has degree two; other gates and
copies have degree three. If n_u inputs are used, sum_v t(v)=2s', so

    copies=s'-n_u+1,
    V=|V(G)|=2s'+1, E=|E(G)|=3s'-n_u+1,
    E-V=s'-n_u.                                     (B7)

Connectivity follows by following ancestor paths to the output. Repeated
pins create distinct parallel pin edges and copies force their common
signal; shared descendants are routed once per node, not unfolded into
separate subcircuits.

For an ordering of G of width w, let E_i be its crossing edges after i
vertices. Construct one program state for every assignment of bits to E_i.
At vertex i, adjacent states must agree on common cut edges and satisfy
the vertex's local rule: at a gate, root value equals its truth table on
the two pins; at output the truth table equals one; at a copy all three
values coincide. An input layer reads that input and labels its edge by
the input's unique incident edge value. Other layers guess.

At a vertex, the union of consecutive cuts is the disjoint union of their
intersection and that vertex's incident edges. A gate has at most four
local satisfying assignments, a copy or input at most two. Every layer
therefore has at most 4*2^w edges, giving

    |P|<=4V*2^w.                                    (B8)

A consistent path gives a well-defined value to every graph edge, since
each edge persists through consecutive cuts and common edges agree.
Conversely any globally consistent edge assignment gives one path. The
input labels, gate rules and copy rules force the unique evaluation of
the original circuit, inductively in its topological order. The output
rule accepts exactly when that evaluation is one. Every used input is
read exactly once, and every unused input is unread. This proof uses the
local table rule only, so covers all sixteen tables with no gate-type loss.

Restrict this compiled program to a valid prefix pair and fixed tail.
Let r be the number of unused payload variables, and suppose (B6)'s
accepting-count assumption holds. Combining (B6) and (B8) gives

    cw(G)>=log2 N1(g)-r-log2((t-1)t!)-2-log2 V.       (B9)

Only the program is restricted, so substitutions creating circuit constants
are never an uncharged step.

## 5. From a subcubic wire graph to a cubic multigraph

Let G be any connected loopless multigraph with maximum degree three,
V vertices and cyclomatic number mu=E-V+1. A subcubic tree on n vertices
has cutwidth at most 2log2 n: choose a centroid z, whose removal has at
most three components of size at most n/2; order one component, then z,
then the other two. Inside a component at most two outside edges cross,
so induction gives 2+2log2(n/2). A centroid exists by repeatedly moving
into a component larger than n/2; the move cannot immediately reverse and
cannot continue in a finite tree.

For mu>=1, iteratively deleting degree-one vertices leaves a nonempty
connected 2-core G_2 with minimum degree two and unchanged E-V. Cycle
vertices cannot disappear; each deleted vertex has exactly one remaining
edge, preserving connectivity and E-V. Each component outside the core
is a tree attached to the core by exactly one edge: charge edges to the
endpoint deleted first, giving at most one charged edge per deleted
vertex, while connectivity requires at least |T|-1 internal edges and
one attachment. Each core vertex has at most one attached tree, since
its core degree is at least two.

If mu>=2, the number D of degree-three core vertices is
2(E(G_2)-V(G_2))=2(mu-1). Suppress degree-two vertices to obtain a connected
cubic multigraph K on those D vertices, retaining loops and parallel
edges. Suppression preserves degree at the remaining vertices and
connectivity. An intermediate degree-two vertex cannot carry a loop,
which would isolate it despite the presence of degree-three vertices.
Bookkeeping associates to each kernel edge its path in G_2; these paths
partition the core's edges and their internal vertex sets are disjoint.
Every kernel loop has at least one internal vertex because G is loopless.

An ordering of K extends to G_2 by inserting the internal vertices of
each kernel path immediately after its earlier endpoint, in path order;
insert a loop's block after its sole endpoint. Nonloop paths contribute
one crossing edge exactly when the corresponding kernel edge crosses
the current kernel cut. At the currently active kernel vertex at most
one loop can occur, and it contributes at most two additional edges.
Thus cw(G_2)<=cw(K)+2. Insert each pendant tree as one block after its
attachment vertex. Inside the block at most its own 2log2 V edges, its
one attachment edge and the unchanged core cut cross. Therefore

    cw(G)<=cw(K)+3+2log2 V,  D=2(mu-1), mu>=2.       (B10)

For mu=1 the core is a single cycle (including the parallel-edge
two-cycle), of cutwidth two; for mu=0 G is a tree. Both give

    cw(G)<=3+2log2 V, mu<=1.                         (B11)

These arguments charge loops, subdivisions, terminal tree cases and all
finite additive losses explicitly.

## 6. Actual lengths, the remainder and strict rounding

For N>=16 the definitions give

    4<=t<=sqrt(N)<2t, sqrt(N)-4<m<2sqrt(N),
    0<=ell<=2t+1, m odd, gcd(m,t)=1.

Indeed (N-t)/(t+1)>sqrt(N)-2, so m0>sqrt(N)-3 and
m>=m0-1>sqrt(N)-4; also m<=N/t<2sqrt(N). The floor leaves a residue at
most t, increased by t+1 if parity requires decrementing m0.
For N>=256, k>=4, t>=16 and m>=2k+3: the function
x-2log2 x is increasing for x>=16 and equals eight there, while
k<=log2(N)/2. Put

    r_max=ceil(log2 t)+ceil(log2(t!+1))-1.

Since t!=product_(j=1)^t j<=t^(t-1),

    r_max<=kt, log2((t-1)t!)<=kt,
    kt<=sqrt(N)log2(N)/2.                            (B12)

The payload has N1(g)>0 and (B4). It cannot be constant or a projection
when N>=256: either would ignore at least tm-1 variables, whereas (B5)
allows only r_max<=kt and tm-1>kt. Thus normalization in Section 4 applies
to every circuit computing L_N. Its unused payload count r<=r_max by
(B5), and n_u>=tm-r. Moreover

    m(t-1)-r_max >= (2k+3)(t-1)-kt
                 >= k(t-1)+1 >= ceil(log2(t!+1)),

so (B3) verifies N1(g)>=2^r(t!+1), exactly the assumption of (B9).

Write Lambda=log2 N1(g)-r-log2((t-1)t!). By (B9),
cw(G)>=Lambda-2-log2 V. For mu>=2, (B10) and H(gamma,h), with
D=2(s'-n_u), imply

    s'-n_u >= [Lambda-5-3log2(2s+1)-h(2s)]/(2gamma).

Here s'<=s, V=2s'+1, D<=2s and monotonicity of h pays the replacements.
For mu<=1, (B11) implies the numerator on the right is nonpositive,
while s'-n_u=mu-1>=-1. Consequently both cases give

    s >= tm-r_max-1
      +[(t-1)m-r_max-log2((t-1)t!)-5
         -3log2(2s+1)-h(2s)]/(2gamma).              (B13)

This deliberately retains the extra -1 needed for trees.

Let s0=ceil((1+1/(2gamma))N). If s>=s0 there is nothing to prove.
Otherwise 2s+1<2s0, h(2s)<=h(2s0), tm=N-t-m-ell and
(t-1)m=N-t-2m-ell. Substitution in (B13) proves

    C_B2(L_N)>=(1+1/(2gamma))N-R_exact(N),
    R_exact=t+m+ell+r_max+1
       +[t+2m+ell+r_max+log2((t-1)t!)+5
          +3log2(2s0)+h(2s0)]/(2gamma).             (B14)

Using the explicit bounds above, one may replace R_exact by

    R(N)=5sqrt(N)+sqrt(N)log2(N)/2+2
       +[7sqrt(N)+sqrt(N)log2(N)+6
          +3log2(2s0)+h(2s0)]/(2gamma).             (B15)

For N<256 set R(N)=(1+1/(2gamma))N, making the bound trivial at those
lengths. All terms in (B15) are o(N): s0=O(N), and
h(2s0)/N=[h(2s0)/(2s0)]*[2s0/N]->0. Thus (B1) holds over every integer
length, with one fixed total P language and no per-length advice. A
strict downward rounding of its coefficient absorbs R(N) eventually and
gives the claimed eventual zero explicit remainder. Neither this argument
nor the graph hypothesis specifies the final threshold.

## 7. An unconditional proof of the KRS bound

We prove: for every field K, shifts a_ij distinct as i varies for each
fixed j, and any b_i, the equations

    f_i(x)=product_(j=1)^t(x_j-a_ij)=b_i, i=1,...,t   (K1)

have at most t! solutions in K^t. Transposing indices gives exactly the
form used in Section 2. Passing to the infinite rational-function field
L=K(T) can only add solutions, so we work over infinite L.

We use these textbook ring facts, stating their content to fix the
dependency: integral elements form a subring, and adjoining finitely many
integral elements gives a finite module; a finite module N with I N=N
has an annihilator 1-a with a in I (determinant trick); a finite module
over a local ring is generated by any lifts of a basis modulo its maximal
ideal (Nakayama); a polynomial ring over a field is a UFD and integrally
closed; if B is integrally closed, the minimal polynomial over Frac(B)
of an element integral over B has all coefficients in B; transcendence
bases of a finitely generated field extension have equal cardinality.
For clarity, the determinant trick follows by writing a finite generating
set as an I-coefficient matrix times itself and multiplying I-matrix by
its adjugate. Nakayama follows by applying that trick to the quotient
by the proposed generators and noting 1-a is a unit in a local ring.
The minimal-polynomial fact follows because its coefficients are
elementary symmetric polynomials in conjugates integral over B, and lie
in Frac(B), hence in B by integral closure. These facts involve no
characteristic, separability, geometric finiteness or generic-fiber
assumption.

Set A=L[x_1,...,x_t], B=L[f_1,...,f_t], M=Frac(A), F=Frac(B), and
g_i^(k)=product_(j!=k)(x_j-a_ij), so f_i=(x_k-a_ik)g_i^(k).

**A ring pigeonhole identity.** In any commutative ring, if n>m and
the differences ell_ij-ell_i'j are units for i!=i', then the products
pi_i=product_(j=1)^m ell_ij generate the unit ideal. Induct on m;
m=0 is immediate. If J=(pi_i), reduction modulo ell_km makes all
ell_im for i!=k units. The n-1 remaining rows in m-1 columns give
J+(ell_km)=R by induction. Hence all ell_km become units in R/J;
there pi_i=0 implies product_(j<m)ell_ij=0 for every i. Apply induction
again in R/J to get 1=0. In particular, applying this to the t rows
and t-1 columns other than k gives polynomials h_i^(k) independent of
x_k with

    sum_i h_i^(k) g_i^(k)=1.                         (K2)

**Finiteness of A over B.** Induct on t; t=1 is immediate. To prove
x_t integral, put x'=(x_1,...,x_(t-1)), g_i=g_i^(t). By induction
L[x'] is integral over C=L[g_1,...,g_(t-1)]. In M put

    y=x_t-a_1t, z=1/y, c_i=a_1t-a_it,
    w=product_i(1+c_i z), S=B[z,w^(-1)].

All denominators are nonzero polynomials in M. The elements
u_i=1/(x_t-a_it)=z/(1+c_i z) lie in zS, so g_i=f_i u_i lies in zS
for every i. In particular C is contained in S. Thus
S'=S[x'] is a finite S-module by induction and integrality. Identity
(K2) gives 1 in zS', hence zS'=S'. The determinant trick on this
finite module gives an annihilator 1-a with a in zS. Since 1 belongs
to S', that annihilator equals zero; therefore 1 is in zS.

Every element of S has the form P(z)/w^k with P in B[Z]. Hence
zP(z)=w^k=1+zQ(z), Q in L[Z], so zU(z)=1 for some U=P-Q in B[Z].
Writing U=sum_(n=0)^D r_n z^n and multiplying by y^(D+1) gives

    y^(D+1)-sum_(n=0)^D r_n y^(D-n)=0.

This is monic over B. Thus y, and x_t=y+a_1t, are integral. Permute
columns to obtain the same conclusion for every x_k. Consequently A
is a finite B-module. This proof is purely algebraic over every field.

**The zero fiber has coordinate-ring dimension t!.** Let I=(f_1,...,f_t)A
and P_k(X)=product_i(X-a_ik). By (K2), P_k(x_k) belongs to I: each
P_k(x_k)g_i^(k) is f_i times a polynomial. Modulo
J=(P_1(x_1),...,P_t(x_t)), monic division leaves a spanning set of t^t
monomials of degrees below t in every variable. Evaluation on the t^t
grid points (a_(tau(1),1),...,a_(tau(t),t)) is onto the product ring
L^(t^t), by the product of one-variable Lagrange interpolation polynomials.
Its kernel contains J and the dimension bound makes the induced map
A/J -> L^(t^t) an isomorphism. The ideal generated there by the f_i
vanishes exactly at grid points where each row index i occurs among
tau(1),...,tau(t), since shifts in each column are distinct. These are
the t! permutations. In a finite product of fields, an ideal generated
by functions consists exactly of the functions vanishing at their common
zeros: multiply any nonzero coordinate value by its coordinate indicator.
Therefore

    A/I is isomorphic to L^(t!), dim_L(A/I)=t!.       (K3)

**Field degree at most t!.** The ideal m=(f_1,...,f_t)B is maximal
with B/m=L: evaluation at (a_11,a_22,...,a_tt) sends every f_i to zero,
and every polynomial in the f_i reduces modulo m to its constant term.
Localize at B minus m. The module A_m is finite over the local ring B_m.
Its quotient A_m/mA_m is the localization of A/I at nonzero constants,
hence has dimension t! over B_m/mB_m=L. Nakayama gives at most t!
generators of A_m over B_m. They span A, hence the F-span F A has
dimension at most t!. This span is a subring of M and a finite-dimensional
domain over F; multiplication by a nonzero element is injective, hence
surjective, so it is a field. It contains A and therefore M=Frac(A).
We conclude

    [M:F]<=t!.                                       (K4)

Since M has transcendence degree t over L and is finite over F, the
t generators f_i of F are algebraically independent: a minimal subset
over which M is algebraic is a transcendence basis and must have t
elements. Thus B is a polynomial ring in the f_i and is integrally
closed.

**Every fiber has at most the field degree.** Let p_1,...,p_r be any
finite set of distinct L-points with f_i(p_k)=b_i for all i,k. Over
infinite L choose a linear form g=sum_j lambda_j x_j taking distinct
values on them: the product over k<l of the nonzero linear forms
sum_j lambda_j(p_kj-p_lj) is a nonzero polynomial, and a nonzero
polynomial over an infinite field has some nonzero value, by induction
using the one-variable root bound. The element g is integral over B;
its minimal polynomial over F is monic of degree n<=[M:F]<=t! and
has coefficients in B. Write those coefficients as polynomials in f_i
and evaluate at p_k. All values g(p_k) satisfy the same monic degree-n
polynomial over L, because the f_i values are the fixed b_i. The
one-variable root bound forces r<=n<=t!. Any t!+1 points would
contradict this argument, so the entire fiber has at most t! points.
This proves (K1) over L and therefore over the original K, for every
right-hand side and without an unproved KRS premise.
