# Norm-one membership, the circuit transfer and scalar covariance bounds

## 1. The language

Reject N<16. For every other actual input length put

```
k=max{j:4^j<=N}, t=2^k,
m=largest odd integer <=floor((N-t)/(t+1)),
ell=N-t-m-tm.
```

Read t coefficients of monic degree-t R(W) over F_2, then m
coefficients of monic degree-m Q(Z). Reject m<1 or a reducible
polynomial. The irreducibility test is divisibility into X^(2^d)-X
and coprimality with X^(2^j)-X for every proper positive divisor
j of d. An irreducible of degree a divides the squarefree
X^(2^j)-X exactly when a|j, by Frobenius order; this proves the
test. It also gives 2^d=sum_(a|d) a I_a for irreducible counts I_a.
The proper-divisor contribution is at most
sum_(a<=d/2)2^a<2^d, so valid headers exist at every degree.

Set F=F_2[Z]/Q, q=2^m and E=F[W]/R. A degree-a irreducible
factor of R over F yields a field of order 2^(ma) containing
the degree-t field generated by its root. Hence t|ma; coprimality
of power-of-two t and odd m forces a=t, so E is a field.
The next tm bits encode z=sum_(u<m,v<t)c_uv Z^u W^v, u fastest,
an additive bijection. Ignore the ell remaining bits. Accept iff
z^e=1 for e=1+q+...+q^(t-1). Polynomial GCD/modular squaring
check the headers in polynomial time; quotient-ring arithmetic
has polynomial bit cost and e has at most N bits. This defines
one total polynomial-time evaluator. Its NP verifier accepts
exactly the empty witness on inputs accepted by the evaluator.

## 2. Many accepts and small rectangles

Fix a valid header and zero tail, obtaining payload function g.
On E*, the image of z->z^e has at most q-1 elements, since
(z^e)^(q-1)=1. Its kernel therefore has at least
(q^t-1)/(q-1)=e points, and the degree-e root bound gives at
most e. Thus N1(g)=e>=2^((t-1)m).

For t distinct d_i in E, the equations (y+d_i)^e=1 become
prod_j(x_j-a_ij)=1 with x_j=y^(q^(j-1)) and
a_ij=-d_i^(q^(j-1)). Each column's shifts are distinct by
Frobenius injectivity. The product-equation lemma below bounds
the solutions by t!; x_1=y makes the original count no larger.
In any payload partition, partial assignments injectively encode
two summands of z. A one-rectangle with t rows and t!+1 columns
would give these forbidden translates. Call this D(t,t!+1),
also allowing partitions with an empty side.

Let S_N be the accepting set on all N bits. Set K_N=1 for N<16
and K_N=2^(t+m+ell)(t!+1) otherwise. A one-rectangle with at
least K_N assignments on each side permits separately fixing
each side's header/tail coordinates, retaining at least t!+1
assignments per side. Together they specify a valid fixed header
and tail, and payload projection on each side is injective.
This contradicts D(t,t!+1). Hence S_N has D(K_N,K_N).

The formulas give sqrt(N)/2<t<=sqrt(N),
sqrt(N)-4<m<2sqrt(N), 0<=ell<=2t+1. Consequently
log_2 K_N=O(sqrt(N)log N)=o(N). One valid header supplies
|S_N|>=2^((t-1)m), so N-log_2|S_N|=O(sqrt(N))=o(N).
These estimates hold over every integer N tending to infinity.

### Product-equation lemma

Over any field K, if a_1j,...,a_tj are distinct for each j,
the equations prod_j(x_j-a_ij)=b_i, 1<=i<=t, have at most t!
solutions, for arbitrary b_i in K and t>=1. This is the form of
[Kollár–Rónyai–Szabó, Theorem 3.3](https://web.vu.lt/mif/s.jukna/boolean/Kollar-norm-graphs.pdf)
needed here; the following supplies its proof.

Over an algebraic closure k, homogenize on X=(P^1_k)^t:

```
F_i=prod_j(u_j-a_ij v_j)-b_i prod_j v_j.
```

No common zero has an infinite coordinate v_j=0. Such coordinates
contribute nonzero first-product factors in every row; each finite
coordinate can kill at most one row by shift distinctness. Fewer
than t finite coordinates cannot kill all t rows. Thus the common
zero scheme Z lies in the affine chart prod_j v_j!=0. Closed in
this chart and in projective X, it is both affine and proper over
k, hence finite ([Stacks 01WN](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01WN)).

The F_i are t sections of L=O_X(1,...,1). At each point of Z,
their local equations cut out dimension zero in the regular,
dimension-t local ring of X. They are a system of parameters,
hence a regular sequence by Cohen–Macaulayness
([02JN](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02JN)). Elsewhere one
equation is a unit. The Koszul complex therefore resolves O_Z,
with L^(-i) repeated binomial(t,i) times in degree i
([062F](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/062F)).

Twist by L^d, d>=t. Projective-line cohomology and the product
formula give chi(X,L^(d-i))=(d-i+1)^t, with higher cohomology
zero. An invertible module on a finite local Artin scheme is free
of rank one, so chi(Z,L^d|Z)=length(Z). Taking Euler
characteristics in the resolution yields

    length(Z)=sum_(i=0)^t (-1)^i binomial(t,i)(d-i+1)^t=t!,

the t-th backward difference of a monic degree-t polynomial.
Each geometric point contributes at least one to the length,
which bounds the original K-points as claimed.
[Projective cohomology](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01XS) ·
[Product formula](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BEC).

## 3. Circuit transfer with actual lengths and zero remainder

All logarithms are base two. Put a_N=N-log|S_N|,
q_N=ceil(log K_N), r_N=2q_N-1. By §2 they are o(N),
and S_N is nonempty eventually. The circuit/program route is
that of McGuire–Schlesinger–Williams (2026); its proof follows.

### Used inputs

A nonempty D(K,K) set, K>=2, ignores at most 2ceil(log K)-1
coordinates. Otherwise vary disjoint ignored subsets of size
ceil(log K) on opposite sides of a partition, keeping an
accepting assignment fixed elsewhere, to obtain a forbidden
rectangle. Any circuit for S_N therefore uses at least N-r_N
inputs. Constant or input-projection outputs are excluded for
all sufficiently large N.

### Read-once programs

A layered nondeterministic program has singleton start/accept
layers and edges to the next layer only. Guess edges are
unlabelled. Each read layer labels its edges by one input
variable's bits, and that variable is read in that layer alone.
An input accepts when some start/accept path agrees with it;
size counts all edges with multiplicity. If it computes nonempty
S with D(K,K), leaves r inputs unread and |S|>=2^r K, then

    |P|>=|S|/[2^r(K-1)^2].                             (3)

Delete unread coordinates, obtaining S' of size |S|/2^r with
the same rectangle property: any forbidden rectangle extends
back by fixing unread coordinates. At node v let A_v,B_v be
read prefixes reaching v and suffixes reaching accept. Their
product is a one-rectangle, so |B_v|>=K implies |A_v|<=K-1.
Along a chosen accepting path, |B_start|=|S'|>=K and
|B_accept|=1<K. Charge the input to the first edge u->v
with |B_v|<K. Its prefix in A_u, suffix in B_v and the fixed
edge label if present reconstruct the input. At most (K-1)^2
inputs charge any edge, proving (3).

### Wire graphs of full-B2 circuits

An N-input size-s circuit is an acyclic shared DAG with one
selected output, two ordered pins per gate, and all sixteen
truth tables. Count every constant, projection, negation and
unary gate once; depth, fan-out and coincident pins are unrestricted.
Inputs, wires and output selection are free. Free constant pins
cause no loss: absorb a constant into the table and feed both
pins by the other feeder; with two constants use a constant table
fed twice by x_1. This adds no gates. Prune nonancestors, leaving
s'<=s gates. Eventually the selected output is a gate, the only
gate of fan-out zero. Let n_u count syntactically used inputs.

For a node v feeding t(v)>=1 pins, distribute its value with
a binary tree of t(v)-1 degree-three copy vertices and t(v)
terminal pin edges; t(v)=1 needs no copy. Each copy equates
its three edge bits. Ordered terminal edges enter the gate pins.
This gives a connected loopless multigraph G, since all nodes
lead to the output and acyclicity precludes self-feeding. Used
inputs have degree one, the output degree two, all other
vertices degree three. There are n_u+s'-1 nodes with outgoing
pins and sum_v t(v)=2s'. Therefore

    copies=s'-n_u+1, V=2s'+1, E=3s'-n_u+1,
    nu(G)=E-V+1=s'-n_u+1.                             (4)

Given any width-w vertex ordering, make program states the
assignments to each cut's edges, counting parallel multiplicity.
At the next vertex, consecutive states agree on common cut
edges and obey its local rule: the gate table, output table
equal to one, or copy equality. Input layers read their variable,
labelling transitions by their incident edge bit; other layers
guess. Empty start/end cuts give singleton layers. The union
of consecutive cuts is their common edges plus the current
vertex's incident edges. At most four assignments satisfy a
gate rule, and at most two satisfy an input/copy rule. Thus

    |P|<=4V*2^w.                                      (5)

Each edge persists between its endpoints, so paths correspond
exactly to global edge assignments satisfying the rules.
Topological induction in the original circuit enforces all
input values, copies and gate evaluations. Acceptance is exactly
S_N, with every used input read once. The unread count
r=N-n_u<=r_N=o(N), and §2 ensures |S_N|>=2^r K_N
eventually, uniformly over circuits. Equations (3),(5) imply

    cw(G)>=log|S_N|-r-2log(K_N-1)-2-log V.             (6)

### Simple cubic to subcubic layouts

Put g=500000000/3827915978. Fix 0<a<g above the limiting
coefficient established in proof.md. Absorb finitely many smaller
orders into C>=0, so every connected simple cubic H satisfies
cw(H)<=a|H|+C. For D=C+9 we prove every connected loopless
subcubic G satisfies

    cw(G)<=2a nu(G)+D ceil(log(V+1))^2.                (7)

First take bridgeless B. Contract adjacent p,q and delete all
r internal parallel edges whenever an endpoint has degree<=2
or r>=2. The new degree is deg(p)+deg(q)-2r<=3; E-V
decreases by r-1. Connectivity, looplessness and bridgelessness
persist: an alternate path avoiding any surviving edge projects
to a walk avoiding it. Termination leaves one vertex or a
connected simple cubic H, with

    |H|/2=E(H)-V(H)<=E(B)-V(B)=nu(B)-1.                (8)

Record each terminal piece's binary contraction tree. Every
piece has original edge boundary<=3, its degree when created.
Order its original leaves depth first, larger child first. In
a proper prefix, earlier siblings on the path to the first missing
leaf occur only when the chosen child has at most half its
parent's leaves. There are at most ceil(log V) such levels;
their pieces have total boundary at most 6ceil(log V). Expanding
an H ordering therefore adds at most this amount to its width:
whole pieces form an H-prefix and only the next piece is partial.
The one-vertex case has the same logarithmic bound. Thus every
bridgeless block B of size at most V has

    cw(B)<=2a nu(B)+6ceil(log V)+C.                    (9)

Delete all bridges of G; the remaining components are bridgeless
blocks, including singletons. Every component F of G-V(B)
attaches to a chosen block B by one bridge: two attachments
would put a purported bridge on a cycle. Edge/vertex counting
gives nu(G)=nu(B)+sum_F nu(F). Insert each F order immediately
before its attachment vertex in a B order. Any cut costs at
most cw(B)+max_F cw(F)+3: only one F can be partially listed,
and crossing attachment bridges meet the next unlisted B
vertex, whose degree is at most three.

Choose a centroid block, every hanging F of size<=|G|/2.
It exists by moving across a bridge into a component larger
than half: the old side becomes smaller than half and any next
large side is a proper subset. For K_V=6ceil(log V)+C+3,
induction on n<=V gives every such subproblem width at most
2a nu(G')+(floor(log n)+1)K_V. Cycle ranks are nonnegative
and additive, hanging orders have size<=n/2, and (9) plus
three bridges costs K_V; singleton/no-attachment cases obey
the same bound. With L=ceil(log(V+1))=floor(log V)+1,
L K_V<=L(6L+C+3)<=(C+9)L^2, proving (7).

### Every sufficiently large integer length

Set c0=4827915978/1000000000=1+1/(2g). If a circuit has
s>=s0=ceil(c0 N), the target already holds. Otherwise set
V0=2s0+1 and L0=ceil(log(V0+1)). Since V<=V0, (4),(6),(7)
give, using n_u>=N-r_N and r<=r_N,

    s>=N-r_N-1
       +[N-a_N-r_N-2log(K_N-1)-2-log V0-D L0^2]/(2a)
      =(1+1/(2a))N-R_N,
    R_N=r_N+1+[a_N+r_N+2log(K_N-1)+2+log V0+D L0^2]/(2a). (10)

All constants a,C,D are fixed before N or the circuit.
By §2 and V0=O(N), R_N=o(N) over every integer length.
The fixed positive gap 1/(2a)-1/(2g) absorbs R_N for all
sufficiently large integers N. Hence

    C_B2(L_N)>=(4827915978/1000000000)N,

with zero subtracted remainder and an unspecified threshold.
Small lengths retain the same total language and verifier.
