# The finite-field input used in this proof

## Interface and provenance

OpenAI, *The matrix multiplication exponent is nine fourths*, November 24, 2026, Theorem 2.2, fixes a finite field, a list length 2^w and record width 2w. For all sufficiently large w and 1≤t≤2^w it bounds below the trivalent volume of any network computing the product of the t independent list-distinctness indicators by t·m_w·w^σ for some field-dependent σ>0. The hypothesis ω<9/4 is attached to the separate compression theorem 2.1. It is absent from the list bound. The paper later uses the two estimates in opposite directions to obtain a contradiction about matrix multiplication.

For (1) in proof.md take the two-element field and one list. Here is the precise alternative normalization and theorem used to establish (1). All symbolic identifiers below refer to the same OpenAI math revision 3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6, under lean/OAI/LinearAlgebra/MatrixExponent/. This is the list theorem itself, independently addressable in the proof library.

* Network.lean builds wire bundles from bit, unit and pair, of widths 1, 0 and the sum of their widths. Values are Booleans, the singleton, or a Cartesian product. The table constructor takes an arbitrary field-valued function on a bundle of input bits and output bits whose total width is at most three; it costs one. Parallel product and composition add the two costs. Composition sums out a shared internal bundle. Rewiring ports and tracing a bundle cost zero. Trace sums over the same value in the input and output ports of that bundle. These are precisely the constructors needed in the last paragraph of the circuit simulation.
* A word is an ordered bundle of bits. The type tree w (word (2*w)) is a balanced tree with 2^w such words as leaves, and flatten lists them in left-to-right order. The one-list input type lists w 1 adds only a unit position. distinctPower with t=1 is the 0/1 indicator that the flattened list has no repetitions, multiplied by the empty-list-product identity 1. Thus distinctPower on one list agrees pointwise with the specified binary predicate.
* The definition Realizes F w t m says that a network to unit computes that pointwise tensor with v vertices for some v and that m=v+t·listBits(w). The latter count includes all exterior bits. ListBounds.lean defines listBits(w)=2w·2^w.
* BatchDescent/Hard.lean, theorem manyList_full, quantifies over every finite field F. It produces η>0, a threshold in w, and at each such w quantifies over all t,m with 1≤t, t≤2^(w^(1+η)), and Realizes F w t m. Its strict conclusion is t·listBits(w)·w^η<m. For F₂ the finiteness and field instances are canonical. For t=1 the upper restriction follows from w^(1+η)≥0. Substituting m=v+listBits(w) proves (1). The file SingleList.lean contains exactly this specialization, without any circuit-hardness premise.

## How hardness enters

The distinguishing mathematical input is hardness of checking tagged permutations, where the tags must form a permutation and each payload must agree with the prescribed output payload at its tagged address. The connection to distinctness can be seen directly for a single list. Write h=w−1 and q=2^h. Given q tags j_i and payloads x_i of h bits each and prescribed payloads y_j, test the tags by distinctness on records (0,j_i,0^(h+1)) together with all the q records (1,j,0^(h+1)). These are 2q words of length 2w. The tags pass precisely for a permutation π.

A second distinctness test starts with the q records (j_i,0,x_i,0). At every address j select a further word, either (j,1,0^h,0) with weight +1 or (j,0,y_j,0) with weight −1. Sum over the independent selector bits. When tags form π, a comparison word is permitted by distinctness exactly when the incoming payload differs from y_j. Its contribution together with the reserve choice is therefore 1−1[different]. The product over addresses is precisely the required equality test at every address. If tags fail, the first tensor gives zero. In characteristic two the same cancellation is 1+1=0. Each selector can be copied and its word specified with O(h) trivalent tables, so two list networks and O(qh) extra vertices realize the whole tagged test. Thus a superlinear lower bound for tagged permutation checking already precludes cheap one-list networks, with at most a constant-factor loss in the size per exterior bit.

We also give the logical structure of the finite-field proof used in the formal evidence. Tagged/Hard.lean requires one positive exponent s for which eventually, at every length parameter h, any network for the square tagged test uses at least 2^h h·h^s vertices. The key contradiction compares two network estimates on the same tensor and scales; their individual hypotheses differ.

1. Diagonal/Bounds.lean defines resistance for a specified collection of additional tables. For any sufficiently large constant C it supplies k and δ>0. At every sufficiently large scale w, N=2^w, some tensor U with at most N exterior bits has a standard network of at most Nw^k vertices. For every 1≤d≤N^δ, every allowed wide implementation of d copies with arity parameter D satisfying D²≤N costs more than CNd. The family U in this intermediate resistance argument may depend on the scale. It is not the final NP language. Processor/Resistance.lean obtains this by a programmable finite-field processor and diagonal resistance; it applies to the particular class of low-cost tables used in the descent.
2. If tagged hardness fails, BatchDescent/Compilation.lean constructs an encoding at arbitrarily large scales and compiles its local operations. BatchDescent/Encoded.lean gives Diagonal.Descent for the same class of additional tables. For constants C≥4 and α>0 and any fixed replica exponent κ>0, at arbitrarily large such w the same compiler works on every network at size M between N and N^(21/20), with e exterior bits. Its first output is an ordinary trivalent realization of d copies for 1≤d≤N^κ, costing at most d(Mw^(−α)+e w^(α/4)), including exterior size. Its second output is a wide realization with D²≤N, replication at most N^κ, and cost at most Cd(Mw^(−α)+e).
3. Choose resistance at cost 2C, b=α/2, and a fixed J with Jb>k+1. Take κ=min(δ,1/40)/J. Since resistance holds eventually and failure of hardness yields arbitrarily large working scales, take one large scale satisfying both. Start with the resistant tensor and its polynomial-in-w overhead network. At a round with t copies write M=v+tN and e≤tN. Resistance applied to the wide output forces Mw^(−α)>tN, since otherwise its cost is at most 2CdtN. Thus the exterior term of ordinary descent is at most Mw^(−α) w^(α/4), and adding tN per copy to the next size costs at most another Mw^(−α). At large w the new overhead per copy is at most M/t times w^(−α/2), since 2+w^(α/4)≤w^(α/2). At J rounds the induction would give v+tN ≤ tN w^(k+1−Jb)<tN, which is impossible. The wide output and the ordinary output in each step may have different replica counts; each obeys the same bound. The total replica count is at most N^(Jκ)≤N^δ. Throughout, padding the size parameter by tN provides M≥N; fixed powers of w and t≤N^(1/40) give M≤N^(21/20) at large w. This contradicts the resistant tensor's strict bound. Diagonal/Broadcast.lean (hard_of_resists) implements the iteration and the arity and copy-budget checks. Processor.hard_of_descent and BatchDescent.hard discharge the two premises. Tagged.manyList_full gives the stated list version.

The supporting derivations are in processor.md for the programmable tensor, and compiler.md for the compression construction. Both fix the identical macro class Cheap(F,50000,2). The later ordinary Boolean circuit simulation uses only the one-list estimate.
